## Introduction 
[Extrusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrusion "Extrusion")** in [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing "Food processing") consists of forcing soft mixed ingredients through an opening in a perforated plate or [die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_(manufacturing) "Die (manufacturing)") designed to produce the required shape. The extruded food is then cut to a specific size by blades. The machine which forces the mix through the die is an **extruder**, and the mix is known as the **extrudate**. The extruder is typically a large, rotating screw tightly fitting within a stationary barrel, at the end of which is the die. In some cases, "extrusion" is taken as synonymous with **extrusion cooking**, which [cooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking "Cooking") the food with heat as it is squeezed through the die.

Extrusion enables [mass production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production "Mass production") of food via a continuous, efficient system that ensures uniformity of the final product. Products made through extrusion (without simultaneous cooking) include [pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasta "Pasta"), [breads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread "Bread") ([croutons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crouton "Crouton"), [bread sticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_stick "Bread stick"), and [flat breads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_bread "Flat bread")), pre-made [cookie dough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_dough "Cookie dough"), and [sausages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sausage "Sausage"). Products made through extrusion cooking include many [breakfast cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_cereal "Breakfast cereal") and ready-to-eat [snacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snack "Snack"), [confectionery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confectionery "Confectionery"), some [baby foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_food "Baby food"), [full-fat soy flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_protein#Flours "Soy protein"), [textured vegetable protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textured_vegetable_protein "Textured vegetable protein"), some [beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverage "Beverage"), and dry and semi-moist [pet foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_food "Pet food"). Food products manufactured using extrusion usually have a high starch content.

## Process
### Extrusion Cooking
In the extrusion cooking process, raw materials are first ground to the correct particle size, usually the consistency of coarse flour. The dry mix is passed through a pre-conditioner, in which other ingredients are added depending on the target product; these may be liquid [sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar "Sugar"), [fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat "Fat"), [dyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dye "Dye"), [meats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat "Meat") or water. [Steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam "Steam") is injected to start the cooking process, and the preconditioned mix (extrudate) is then passed through an extruder. The extruder is a large, [rotating screw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes%27_screw "Archimedes' screw") tightly fitting within a stationary barrel, at the end of which is the die.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1) The extruder's rotating screw forces the extrudate towards and through the die. The extrudate is in the extruder for the **residence time**.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

Many extruded products puff and change texture as they are extruded because of the reduction of forces and release of moisture and heat.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1) The extent to which it does so is known as the **expansion ratio**. The extrudate is cut to the desired length by blades at the output of the extruder, which rotate about the die openings at a specific speed. The product is then cooled and dried, becoming rigid while maintaining porosity.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

Cooking takes place within the extruder, where the product produces its own friction and heat due to the [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure "Pressure") generated (10–20 bar). The process can induce both [protein denaturation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_denaturation "Protein denaturation") and [starch gelatinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch_gelatinization "Starch gelatinization") under some conditions.

Many food extrusion processes involve a high temperature for a short time.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1) Important factors of the extrusion process are the composition of the extrudate, screw length and rotating speed, barrel temperature and moisture, die shape, and rotating speed of the blades. These are controlled based on the desired product to ensure uniformity of the output.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

Moisture is the most important of these factors, and affects the mix [viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity "Viscosity"), acting to plasticize the extrudate. Increasing moisture will decrease viscosity, torque, and product temperature, and increase bulk density. This will also reduce the pressure at the die. Most extrusion processes for food processing are carried out at low to intermediate moisture (moisture level below 40%). High-moisture extrusion is known as **wet extrusion**, but it was not used much before the introduction of twin screw extruders (TSE), which have a more efficient conveying capability. The most important [rheological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheometry "Rheometry") factor in the wet extrusion of high-starch extrudate is temperature.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-akdogan-2)

The amount of salt in the extrudate may determine the colour and texture of some extruded products. The expansion ratio and airiness of the product depend on the salt [concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration "Concentration") in the extrudate, possibly as a result of a chemical reaction between the salt and the starches in the extrudate. Colour changes as a result of salt concentration may be caused by "the ability of salt to change the water activity of the extrudate and thus change the rate of browning reactions". Salt is also used to distribute minor ingredients, such as [food colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_colouring "Food colouring") and [flavours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasoning "Seasoning"), after extrusion; these are more evenly distributed over the product's surface after being mixed with salt.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-strategies-3)

## History
The first extruder was designed to manufacture [sausages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sausage "Sausage") in the 1870s.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-karwe-4)[Dry pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_pasta_line "Dry pasta line") has been produced by extrusion since the 1930s,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-akdogan-2) and the method has been applied to [tater tots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tater_tots "Tater tots") (first extruded potato product: [Ore-Ida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore-Ida "Ore-Ida") in 1953).[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-karwe-4) Some domestic [kitchen appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchen_appliance "Kitchen appliance") such as [meat grinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_grinder "Meat grinder") and some types of pasta makers use extrusion. [Pastry bags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastry_bag "Pastry bag") (piping bags), squeezed by hand, operate by extrusion.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

The first extrusion cooking machine was the expanding pelleting machine from Wenger Mixer Manufacturing from 1954. Its first mentioned use seems to be with [Purina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestl%C3%A9_Purina_PetCare "Nestlé Purina PetCare") in 1957, which developed extruded food for dogs, monkeys, and fish.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-karwe-4)[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-soyinfo-5) In 1963, the USDA and UNICEF tested a full fat soy flour produced from extrusion-cooked soybeans as a source of nutrients for children. Milk substitutes were later developed from this flour. In 1966, the US government started providing a CSM (Corn-Soya-Milk) formula to protein-deficient children in the Third World. The later [Meals for Millions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meals_for_Millions "Meals for Millions") project also prominently featured soy flour in its Multi-Purpose Food (MPF), a high-protein food supplement that could be made for just three cents per meal.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-soyinfo-5)

The idea of using extrusion cooking to produce [breakfast cereal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_cereal "Breakfast cereal") has been mentioned since the Wegner patent of 1960. In 1970, the Israeli Shefa Protein Industries introduced a line of breakfast cereal called Krunch, made from cereal flour and full-fat soy flour. It's unclear whether there has been an earlier breakfast cereal made from extruded products.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-soyinfo-5) Meat analogues have been made through extrusion since 1969.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-soyinfo-5)

## Effects
Extrusion enables mass production of food via a continuous, efficient system that ensures uniformity of the final product. This is achieved by controlling various aspects of the extrusion process. It has also enabled the production of new processed food products and "revolutionized many conventional snack manufacturing processes".[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-riaz-6)

### Chemical changes with cooking

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Food_extrusion&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Chemical changes with cooking")]

Extrusion cooking results in "chemical reactions that occur within the extruder barrel and at the die" like most other forms of [cooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking "Cooking").[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-camire-7) Extrusion enables mass production of some food, and will "denature antinutritional factors"[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1) while destroying [toxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxin "Toxin") or killing [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism"). It may also improve protein quality and digestibility[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1) and affects the product's shape, texture, colour, and flavour.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1) Changes associated with extrusion include:

*   Destruction of certain naturally occurring [toxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxin "Toxin") and [antinutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinutrient "Antinutrient") (including [trypsin inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trypsin_inhibitor "Trypsin inhibitor"), [haemagglutinins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemagglutinin "Haemagglutinin"), [tannins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannin "Tannin") and [phytates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytate "Phytate"))[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-singh-8)
    *   All four listed antinutrients reduce the absorption of protein. Phytate and tannins also reduce the absorption of minerals.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-singh-8)

*   Reduction in the level of [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism") in the final product.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1)
*   Partial destruction of heat-liable vitamins (A, B, C, and E).[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-singh-8)
*   Moderate increase in [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") digestibility, due to protein [denaturation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denaturation_(biochemistry) "Denaturation (biochemistry)") and the inactivation of antinutrients.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-singh-8)
*   [Maillard reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction "Maillard reaction"), which reduce the available amounts of certain amino acids, including the [essential amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_amino_acid "Essential amino acid")[lysine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysine "Lysine").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-PMID_707920-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-Guy-10) Lysine loss can be reduced by using wetter mixtures.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-camire-7)
*   Breakdown of complex carbohydrates ([starches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch "Starch") and [non-starch polysaccharides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-starch_polysaccharide "Non-starch polysaccharide")) into simpler components. Part of this action is caused by amylase from the cereal themselves.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-akdogan-2)
    *   This increases [glycemic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_index "Glycemic index") and creates starches more likely to cause [insulin resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin_resistance "Insulin resistance"). The "extrusion process significantly increased the availability of carbohydrates for digestion".[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-brenner2-11)
    *   This may also lead to higher [tooth decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooth_decay "Tooth decay").
    *   On the other hand, this breakdown converts insoluble fibers into soluble fibers.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-camire-7)

*   Binding and volatization of flavor compounds.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-camire-7)
*   Gelatinization of starch.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-camire-7)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-riaz82-12)
*   An increase in iron content due to the wearing of machine components. No significant change in zinc absorption.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-camire-7)
*   As of 1998, little is known about the stability or bioavailability of [phytochemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytochemical "Phytochemical") involved in extrusion. Phenols appear to be decreased.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-camire-7)

Overall, the effects of "extrusion cooking on nutritional quality are ambiguous",[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-singh-8) as extrusion may change carbohydrates, dietary fibre, the protein and amino acid profile, vitamins, and mineral content of the extrudate in a manner that is beneficial or harmful.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-singh-8) Nutritional quality has been found to improve with moderate conditions (short duration, high moisture, low temperature), whereas a negative effect on nutritional quality of the extrudate occurs with a high temperature (at least 200°C), low moisture (less than 15%), or improper components in the mix.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-singh-8) High-temperature extrusion for a short duration "minimizes losses in vitamins and amino acids".[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1)

A 2012 research paper indicates that use of non-traditional cereal flours, such as [amaranth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaranth "Amaranth"), [buckwheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckwheat "Buckwheat") or [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet "Millet"), may be used to reduce the [glycemic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_index "Glycemic index") of breakfast cereals produced by extrusion.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-brenner-13) The extrudate using these cereal flours exhibits a higher bulk and product density, has a similar expansion ratio, and has "a significant reduction in readily digestible carbohydrates and slowly digestible carbohydrates".[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-brenner-13) A 2008 paper states that replacing 5% to 15% of the [wheat flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_flour "Wheat flour") and white flour with [dietary fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_fibre "Dietary fibre") in the extrudate breakfast cereal mix significantly reduces "the rate and extent of carbohydrate [hydrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolysis "Hydrolysis") of the extruded products",[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-brenner2-11) which increased the level of slowly digested carbohydrates and reduced the level of quickly digested carbohydrates.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-brenner2-11)

### Texture
The material of which an extrusion die is made can affect the final product. Rough [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze "Bronze") dies on pasta extruders produce a rougher surface than smooth [stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainless_steel "Stainless steel") dies, considered to make more liquid pasta sauces adhere better;[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-14) pasta made this way is labelled "bronze die" pasta to indicate a premium product.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-15)

## Products
Extrusion has enabled the production of new processed food products and "revolutionized many conventional snack manufacturing processes".[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-riaz-6)

The various types of food products manufactured by extrusion typically have a high starch content.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-harper-1)_Directly expanded_ types include [breakfast cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_cereal "Breakfast cereal") and corn curls, and are made in high-temperature, low-moisture conditions under [high shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_rate "Shear rate"). _Unexpanded_ products include [pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasta "Pasta"), which is produced at intermediate moisture (about 40%) and low temperature. _Texturized_ products include [meat analogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_analogue "Meat analogue"), which are made using plant proteins ("textured vegetable protein") and a long die to "impart a fibrous, meat-like structure to the extrudate",[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-karwe-4) and [fish paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_paste "Fish paste").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-HandH-16)

Some [processed cheeses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processed_cheese "Processed cheese") and [cheese analogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_analogue "Cheese analogue") are also made by extrusion. Processed cheeses extruded with low moisture and temperature "might be better suited for manufacturing using extrusion technology" than those at high moisture or temperature. Lower moisture cheeses are firmer and chewier, and [cheddar cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheddar_cheese "Cheddar cheese") with low moisture and an extrusion temperature of 80°C was preferred by subjects in a study to other extruded cheddar cheese produced under different conditions.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-jsfa-17) An extrudate mean residence time of about 100 seconds can produce "processed cheeses or cheese analogues of varying texture (spreadable to sliceable)".[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-18)

Confectionery made via extrusion includes [chewing gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chewing_gum "Chewing gum"), [liquorice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquorice "Liquorice"), and [toffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toffee "Toffee").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-HandH-16) Other food products often produced by extrusion include some [breads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread "Bread") ([croutons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crouton "Crouton"), [bread sticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_stick "Bread stick"), and [flat breads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_bread "Flat bread")), various ready-to-eat [snacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snack "Snack"), pre-made [cookie dough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_dough "Cookie dough"), some [baby foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_food "Baby food"), some [beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverage "Beverage"), and dry and semi-moist [pet foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_food "Pet food"). Specific examples include cheese curls, [macaroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaroni "Macaroni"), [Fig Newtons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fig_Newtons "Fig Newtons"), [jelly beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelly_bean "Jelly bean"), [sevai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevai "Sevai"), and some [french fries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_fries "French fries").[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_extrusion#cite_note-19)

Extrusion is also used to [modify starch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_starch "Modified starch") and to pellet [animal feed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_feed "Compound feed").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
