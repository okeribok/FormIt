**Marcus Porcius Cato** (; 234–149 BC), also known as **Cato the Censor** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language "Latin language"): _Censorius_), **the Elder** and **the Wise**, was a Roman soldier, [senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Senate "Roman Senate"), and [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_historiography "Roman historiography") known for his [conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism "Conservatism") and opposition to [Hellenization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization "Hellenization").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-eb-2) He was the first to [write history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_history#Roman_world "History of history") in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") with his _[Origines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origines "Origines")_, a now fragmentary work on the [history of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rome "History of Rome"). His work _[De agri cultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De\_agri\_cultura "De agri cultura")_, a treatise on agriculture, rituals, and recipes, is the oldest extant prose written in the Latin language. Cato's [epithet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithet "Epithet") "Elder" distinguishes him from his great-grandson [Cato the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Younger "Cato the Younger").

Cato came from an ancient [plebeian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebs "Plebs") family who were noted for their [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_army "Roman army") service. As a youth, he fought as a [military tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tribune "Military tribune") in the [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War "Second Punic War") with distinction. Like his forefathers, when not serving in the army, Cato was devoted to [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_agriculture "Roman agriculture"). Having attracted the notice of patrician [Lucius Valerius Flaccus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Valerius_Flaccus_(consul_195_BC) "Lucius Valerius Flaccus (consul 195 BC)"), he was brought to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") and began his political career. Cato was successively [quaestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaestor "Quaestor") (204), [aedile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aedile "Aedile") (199), [praetor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetor "Praetor") (198), and in 195 he was elected [consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_consul "Roman consul") with Flaccus. As consul, he unsuccessfully opposed the repeal of the _[Lex Oppia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex\_Oppia "Lex Oppia")_, a law restricting female luxury. His subsequent military campaign and administrative efforts in [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania "Hispania") proved a major success, and on his return to Rome in 194 BC he was rewarded with a [triumph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_triumph "Roman triumph").

Following the end of his consulship, Cato served with distinction as a [legate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legatus "Legatus") at the [Battle of Thermopylae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thermopylae_(191_BC) "Battle of Thermopylae (191 BC)") against [Seleucid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire") king [Antiochus III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochus_III_the_Great "Antiochus III the Great"). In 184 he was elected [censor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_censor "Roman censor"), shortly after his attacks forced [Scipio Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Africanus "Scipio Africanus") into retirement. As censor, he introduced taxes on luxuries and oversaw extensive constructions and repairs of public works. Seeking to preserve [Rome's ancestral customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mos_maiorum "Mos maiorum"), he strongly opposed [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period "Hellenistic period") influences. Later in his life, he also became known for his vigorously anti-[Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage") stance.

[![Image 2](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Tuscolo1.jpg/250px-Tuscolo1.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tuscolo1.jpg)

The theatre at [Tusculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusculum "Tusculum")

Cato the Elder was born in the municipal town of [Tusculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusculum "Tusculum"), like some generations of his ancestors. His father had earned a reputation as a brave soldier, and his great-grandfather had received a reward from the state for having had five horses killed under him in battle. However, the Tusculan [Porcii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcii "Porcii") had never obtained the privileges of the [Roman magistracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_magistrate "Roman magistrate"). Cato the Elder, their famous descendant, at the beginning of his career in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rome "History of Rome"), was regarded as a _[novus homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novus\_homo "Novus homo")_ (new man), and the feeling of his unsatisfactory position, working along with the belief of his inherent superiority, aggravated and drove his ambition. Early in life, he so far exceeded the previous deeds of his predecessors that he is frequently spoken of not only as the leader, but as the founder of the [Porcia gens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcia_gens "Porcia gens").

His ancestors for three generations had been named Marcus Porcius, and it was said by [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch "Plutarch") that at first he was known by the additional [cognomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognomen "Cognomen")_Priscus_, but was afterwards called Cato—a word (from Latin _catus_) indicating 'common sense that is the result of natural wisdom combined with experience'.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-3) Priscus, like _Major_, may have been merely an epithet used to distinguish him from the later [Cato the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Younger "Cato the Younger").

There is no precise information as to when he first received the title of Cato, which may have been given in childhood as a symbol of distinction. The qualities implied in the word Cato were acknowledged by the plainer and less outdated title of _Sapiens_, by which he was so well known in his old age that [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero "Cicero") says it became his virtual _cognomen_.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-4) From the number and eloquence of his speeches, he was a gifted orator,[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-6) but Cato the Censor (_Cato Censorius_), and Cato the Elder are now his most common, as well as his most characteristic names, since he carried out the office of Censor with extraordinary standing and was the only Cato who ever held it.

### Deducing Cato's date of birth

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Deducing Cato's date of birth")]

The date of Cato's birth has to be deduced from conflicting reports of his age at the time of his death, which is known to have happened in 149 BC. According to the chronology of Cicero, Cato was born in 234 BC, in the year before the first Consulship of [Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Fabius_Maximus_Verrucosus "Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus"),[[n 1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-8) and died at the age of 85, in the consulship of Lucius Marcius Censorinus and Manius Manilius. Pliny[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-9) agrees with [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero "Cicero"). Other authors exaggerate the age of Cato. According to [Valerius Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerius_Maximus "Valerius Maximus")[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-10) he survived his 86th year, according to Livy[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-11) and Plutarch[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-12) he was 90 years old when he died. These exaggerated ages, however, are inconsistent with a statement of Cato himself that is recorded by Plutarch.[[n 2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-13)

[![Image 3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Heinrich_Leutemann_-_Hannibals_%C3%9Cbergang_%C3%BCber_die_Alpen_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Heinrich_Leutemann_-_Hannibals_%C3%9Cbergang_%C3%BCber_die_Alpen_%28cropped%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Heinrich_Leutemann_-_Hannibals_%C3%9Cbergang_%C3%BCber_die_Alpen_(cropped).jpg)

[Hannibal and his men crossing the Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal%27s_crossing_of_the_Alps "Hannibal's crossing of the Alps")

When Cato was very young, after his father's death, he inherited a small property in [Sabine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabine "Sabine") territory, at a distance from his native town. There, he spent most of his childhood overseeing the operations of the farm, learning business and the rural economy. Near this land was a small hut owned by [Manius Curius Dentatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manius_Curius_Dentatus "Manius Curius Dentatus"), whose military feats and rigidly simple character were remembered and admired in the neighborhood. Cato was inspired to imitate that character, hoping to match the glory of Dentatus.

Soon an opportunity came for a military campaign. In 218 BC [Hannibal Barca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal "Hannibal") attacked one of Rome's allies, starting the [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War "Second Punic War"). Experts express some disagreement about Cato's early military life. In 214, he served at [Capua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capua "Capua"), and the historian Wilhelm Drumann imagines that already, at the age of 20, he was a [military tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tribune "Military tribune").[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-14)[Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Fabius_Maximus_Verrucosus "Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus") had the command in this area, [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania "Campania"), during the year of his fourth consulship.

At the siege of [Tarentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taranto "Taranto"), in 209, Cato was again at the side of Fabius. Two years later, Cato was one of the men who went with the consul Claudius Nero on his northern march from [Lucania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucania "Lucania") to check the progress of [Hasdrubal Barca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasdrubal_Barca "Hasdrubal Barca"). It is recorded that the services of Cato contributed to the decisive and important victory of Sena at the [Battle of the Metaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Metaurus "Battle of Metaurus"), where Hasdrubal was slain.

Cato later gave several vehement speeches, which he often ended by saying "_[Carthago delenda est](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthago\_delenda\_est "Carthago delenda est")_", or "Carthage must be destroyed."[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-15)

In the pauses between campaigns Cato returned to his Sabine farm, where he dressed simply, working and behaving like his laborers. Young as he was, the neighboring farmers liked his tough mode of living, enjoyed his old-fashioned and concise proverbs, and had a high regard for his abilities. His own active personality made him willing and eager to make himself available in the service of his neighbors. He was selected to act, sometimes as an arbitrator of disputes, and sometimes as a supporter in local causes, which were probably tried in front of _recuperatores_, the judges for causes of great public interest. Consequently, he was enabled to strengthen by practice his oratorical abilities, to gain self-confidence, to observe the manners of men, to analyze the diversity of human nature, to apply the rules of law, and to practically investigate the principles of justice.

#### Follower of the old Roman strictness

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Follower of the old Roman strictness")]

In the area surrounding Cato's Sabine farm were the lands of [Lucius Valerius Flaccus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Valerius_Flaccus_(consul_195_BC) "Lucius Valerius Flaccus (consul 195 BC)"), a young nobleman of significant influence and high patrician family. Flaccus could not help remarking on Cato's energy, his military talent, his eloquence, his frugal and simple life, and his traditional principles. Flaccus himself was a member of that purist patrician faction which displayed its adherence to the stricter virtues of the Roman character.

Within Roman society a transition was in progress—from [Samnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samnium "Samnium") rusticity to Grecian civilization and oriental luxuriance. The chief [magistracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistratus "Magistratus") of the state had become almost hereditary for a few wealthy and upper-class families. They were popular by acts of generosity and charming manners, and they collected material wealth from their clients and followers, as well as intellectual prowess provided by their education, taste in the fine arts, and knowledge of literature.

Nonetheless, the less fortunate nobles, envious of this exclusive oligarchy and critical of the decadence and luxury, formed a party with a more conservative and ascetic ideology. In their eyes, rusticity and austerity were the marks of Sabine character, and of the old Roman inflexible integrity and love of order. [Marcus Claudius Marcellus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Claudius_Marcellus "Marcus Claudius Marcellus"), [Scipio Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Africanus "Scipio Africanus") and his family, and [Titus Quinctius Flamininus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Quinctius_Flamininus "Titus Quinctius Flamininus") may be taken as representative of the new culture; Cato's friends, Fabius and Flaccus, were the leading men in the faction defending the old plainness.

#### Path to magistracies

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Path to magistracies")]

[![Image 4](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/Roman.forum.%26.arch.of.septimius.rome.arp.jpg/250px-Roman.forum.%26.arch.of.septimius.rome.arp.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Roman.forum.%26.arch.of.septimius.rome.arp.jpg)

Part of the Roman Forum. The arch was erected by [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_of_Septimius_Severus "Arch of Septimius Severus").

Flaccus was a perceptive politician, who looked for young and emergent men to support him. He had observed Cato's martial spirit and heard his eloquent tongue. He knew how much courage and persuasiveness were valued at Rome. He also knew that distinction achieved on the battlefield opened the way to achievements in the higher civil offices. Flaccus knew too that for a stranger like Cato, the only way to the magisterial honours was success in the [Roman Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum "Roman Forum").[[n 3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-17)

For that reason, he suggested to Cato that he shift his ambition to the field of Roman politics. The advice was followed. Invited to the townhouse of Flaccus, and ratified by his support, Cato began to distinguish himself in the forum, and became a candidate for assuming a post in the magistracy.

### Early military career

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Early military career")]

In 205 BC, Cato was appointed [quaestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaestor "Quaestor"), and in the next year (204) he entered upon the duties of his place of work, following [Publius Cornelius Scipio Africanus Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Africanus "Scipio Africanus") to Sicily. When Scipio, after much opposition, obtained from the Senate permission to transport armed forces from Sicily to [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Province "Africa Province"), Cato and [Gaius Laelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Laelius "Gaius Laelius") were appointed to escort the baggage ships. Yet there proved not to be the friendliness of cooperation between Cato and Scipio which ought to have existed between a quaestor and his [proconsul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proconsul "Proconsul").

Fabius had opposed the permission given to Scipio to carry the attack to the enemy's home, and Cato, whose appointment was intended to monitor Scipio's behavior, adopted the views of his friend. Plutarch reports that the lenient discipline of the troops under Scipio's command and the exaggerated expenses incurred by the general provoked Cato's protests, such that Scipio, immediately afterward, replied angrily, saying he would give an account of victories, not of money.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-18)

Cato left his place of duty after the dispute with Scipio about the latter's alleged extravagance, and returning to Rome, condemned the uneconomical activities of his general to the senate. Plutarch went on to say that at the joint request of Cato and Fabius, a commission of tribunes was sent to Sicily to examine Scipio's activity. Upon their review of his extensive and careful arrangements for the transport of the troops, they determined he was not guilty of Cato's charges.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-19)

Plutarch's version, which seemed to attribute to Cato the wrongdoing of quitting his post before his time, is barely consistent with Livy's narrative. If Livy is correct, the commission was sent because of the complaints of the inhabitants of Locri, who had been harshly oppressed by [Quintus Pleminius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Pleminius "Quintus Pleminius"), Scipio's legate. Livy says nothing of Cato's interference in this matter, but mentions the bitterness with which Fabius blamed Scipio for corrupting military discipline and for having illegally left his province to take the town of [Locri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locri "Locri").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-20)

The author of the abridged life of Cato, commonly considered the work of [Cornelius Nepos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Nepos "Cornelius Nepos"), asserts that Cato, after his return from Africa, put in at [Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinia "Sardinia"), and brought the poet [Quintus Ennius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Ennius "Quintus Ennius") in his own ship from the island to Italy. But because Sardinia is rather out of the line of the trip to Rome, it is more likely that the first contact between Ennius and Cato happened at a later date, when the latter was [praetor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetor "Praetor") in Sardinia.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-ReferenceA-21)

In 199 BC Cato was elected [aedile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aedile "Aedile"), and with his colleague Helvius, restored the Plebeian Games, and gave upon that occasion a banquet in honour of [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_(mythology) "Jupiter (mythology)"). In 198 BC he was elected [praetor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetor "Praetor"), and obtained Sardinia as his province, with the command of 3,000 infantry and 200 cavalry. Here he took the earliest opportunity to demonstrate his main beliefs by practicing his strict public morality. He reduced official operating costs, walked his trips with a single assistant, and placed his own frugality in contrast with the opulence of provincial magistrates. The rites of religion were celebrated with thrift, justice was administered with strict [impartiality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impartiality "Impartiality"), and [usury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usury "Usury") was severely punished. According to Aurelius Victor, a revolt in Sardinia was subdued by Cato during his praetorship.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-ReferenceA-21)

#### Enactment of the Porcian Laws

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Enactment of the Porcian Laws")]

In 195, when he was only 39 years old, Cato was elected [junior consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_consul "Junior consul") to his old friend and patron Flaccus. During his consulship, he enacted the first two of the [Porcian Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcian_Laws "Porcian Laws"), which expanded the protections of [Roman citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_citizen "Roman citizen") against degrading or capricious punishment under the Republic's [Valerian Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerian_Law "Valerian Law").

#### Repeal of the Oppian Law

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Repeal of the Oppian Law")]

In 215, at the height of the Second Punic War and at the request of the [tribune of the plebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribune_of_the_plebs "Tribune of the plebs") Gaius Oppius, the [Oppian Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppian_Law "Oppian Law") (_Lex Oppia_), intended to restrict the luxury and extravagance of women in order to save money for the public treasury, was passed. The law specified that no woman could own more than half an ounce of gold, nor wear a garment of several colours, nor drive a carriage with horses closer than a mile to the city, except to attend public celebrations of religious rites.

After [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal "Hannibal") was defeated and [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") was resplendent with [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage "Ancient Carthage") wealth, tribunes Marcus Fundanius and Lucius Valerius proposed to abolish the Oppian law, but tribunes Marcus Junius Brutus and Titus Junius Brutus opposed doing so. This conflict spawned far more interest than the most important state affairs. Middle-aged married Roman women crowded the streets, blocked access to the forum, and intercepted their approaching husbands, demanding to restore the traditional ornaments of Roman matrons.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-22)

They even begged the praetors, consuls and other magistrates. Even Flaccus hesitated, but his colleague Cato was inflexible, and made a characteristically impolite speech, which was later retold by Livy.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-23) The dissenting tribunes withdrew their opposition and the Oppian law was repealed by vote of all tribes. Women went in procession through the streets and the forum, dressed up with their now legitimate finery.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-24)

During the controversy Cato maintained a firm opposition to the repeal, so he suffered politically and personally when it was finally repealed. Not only had the former consul been rejected by the senate by unanimous decision, but Flaccus failed to stand with him.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-Bauman_1983_159-25) However, perhaps because of Flaccus' connection to Lucius Valerius he was deliberately staying out of the controversy.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-Bauman_1983_159-25) He soon set sail for his appointed province, [Hispania Citerior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania_Citerior "Hispania Citerior").

#### Post in Hispania Citerior

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Post in Hispania Citerior")]

In his campaign in [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania "Hispania"), Cato behaved in keeping with his reputation of untiring hard work and alertness. He lived soberly, sharing the food and the labours of the common soldier. Wherever it was possible, he personally superintended the execution of his orders. His movements were reported as bold and rapid, and he always pushed for victory. His operations appear to have been carefully designed, and were coordinated with the plans of other generals in other parts of Hispania. His manoeuvres were considered original and successful. He managed to benefit by setting tribe against tribe, and took native mercenaries into his pay.

[![Image 5](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Hispania_1a_division_provincial.PNG/250px-Hispania_1a_division_provincial.PNG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hispania_1a_division_provincial.PNG)

[Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania "Hispania") in 197 BC

The details of the campaign, as related by Livy,[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-26) and illustrated by incidental anecdotes by [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch "Plutarch"), are full of horror and they make clear that Cato reduced [Hispania Citerior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania_Citerior "Hispania Citerior") to subjection with great speed and little mercy. We read of multitudes who put themselves to death because of the dishonour after they had been stripped of all their arms, of extensive massacres of surrendered troops, and the frequent harsh plunders. The phrase [bellum se ipsum alet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellum_se_ipsum_alet "Bellum se ipsum alet")—the war will feed itself—was coined by Cato during this period.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-27) His conduct in Hispania were not contradictory with the traditional ideals of a Roman soldier, or with his own firm and over-assertive temper. He claimed to have destroyed more towns in Hispania than he had spent days in that country.

After he reduced the area between the River [Iberus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebro "Ebro") and the [Pyrenees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenees "Pyrenees") to a resentful and, as it turned out, temporary obedience, Cato turned his attention to administrative reforms, and increased the revenues of the province by improvements in the working of the [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron") and [silver mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_mine "Silver mine").

For his achievements in Hispania, the senate decreed a thanksgiving ceremony of three days. In the course of the year 194 BC, he returned to Rome and was rewarded with the honour of a [Roman triumph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_triumph "Roman triumph"), at which he exhibited an extraordinary quantity of captured [brass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass "Brass"), silver, and [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold "Gold"), both coin and ingots. Cato distributed the monetary prize to his soldiery, and was more liberal than might have been expected from his vigorous parsimony.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-28)

The return of Cato seems to have accelerated the enmity of [Scipio Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Africanus "Scipio Africanus"), who was Consul in 194 BC and is said to have desired the command of the province in which Cato was harvesting notoriety. There is some disagreement between Nepos (or the pseudo-Nepos), and [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch "Plutarch"),[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-29) in their accounts of this topic. Nepos claims that Scipio failed to obtain the province, and, offended by the rejection, remained after his consulship in a private capacity at Rome. Plutarch claims that Scipio, who was disgusted by Cato's severity, was appointed to succeed him but could not convince the senate to censure Cato's administration, and passed his consulship in inactivity. Plutarch was probably mistaken, judging by the statement in [Livy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livy "Livy"),[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-30) that in 194 BC, Sextus Digitius was appointed to the province of [Hispania Citerior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania_Citerior "Hispania Citerior"). The notion that Scipio was appointed successor to Cato in Hispania may have arisen from a double confusion of name and place, since [Publius Cornelius Scipio Nasica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publius_Cornelius_Scipio_Nasica_(consul_191_BC) "Publius Cornelius Scipio Nasica (consul 191 BC)") was chosen in 194 BC to the province of [Hispania Ulterior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania_Ulterior "Hispania Ulterior").

However true this account, Cato used his eloquence and produced detailed financial accounts to successfully defend against criticism of his consulship. The known fragments of the speeches (or one speech under different names) made after his return attest to the strength of his arguments.

Plutarch[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-31) states that, after his consulship, Cato accompanied [Tiberius Sempronius Longus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius_Sempronius_Longus_(consul_194_BCE) "Tiberius Sempronius Longus (consul 194 BCE)") as [legatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legatus "Legatus") to [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace "Thrace"), but this seems incorrect because, although Scipio Africanus believed that one consul should have [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedon "Macedon"), Sempronius was soon in [Cisalpine Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Gaul "Cisalpine Gaul"),[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-32) and in 193 BC Cato was in Rome dedicating a small temple to [Victoria Virgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_(mythology) "Victoria (mythology)").[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-33)

### Late military career

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Late military career")]

#### Battle of Thermopylae

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Battle of Thermopylae")]

The military career of Cato had not yet ended. In 191, he, along with his old associate [Lucius Valerius Flaccus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Valerius_Flaccus_(consul_195_BC) "Lucius Valerius Flaccus (consul 195 BC)"), were appointed as lieutenant-generals ([legatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legatus "Legatus")) under the consul [Manius Acilius Glabrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manius_Acilius_Glabrio_(consul_191_BC) "Manius Acilius Glabrio (consul 191 BC)"), who had been dispatched to [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_Greece "Hellenistic Greece") to oppose the invasion of [Antiochus III the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochus_III_the_Great "Antiochus III the Great"), King of the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire"). In the decisive [Battle of Thermopylae (191 BC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thermopylae_(191_BC) "Battle of Thermopylae (191 BC)"), which led to the downfall of Antiochus, Cato behaved with his usual valor, and enjoyed good fortune. By a daring and difficult advance, he surprised and defeated a body of the enemy's [Aetolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aetolia "Aetolia")[auxiliaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliaries "Auxiliaries"), who were posted upon the Callidromus, the highest peak of the range of [Mount Oeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Oeta "Mount Oeta").[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-34)

Then, coming to the aid of forces under Flaccus's command, he began a sudden descent from the hills above the royal camp, and the panic caused by this unexpected movement promptly turned the day in favor of the Romans, and signaled the end of the Seleucid invasion of Greece. After the action, the consul hugged Cato with the greatest warmth and attributed to him the whole credit of the victory. This fact rests on the authority of Cato himself, who, like [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero "Cicero"), often indulged in the habit, offensive to modern taste, of sounding his own praises.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-35)

After an interval spent in the pursuit of Antiochus and the pacification of Greece, Cato was sent to Rome by Glabrio to announce the successful outcome of the campaign, and he performed his journey with such celerity that he had started his report in the senate before the return of [Lucius Cornelius Scipio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Cornelius_Scipio_Asiaticus "Lucius Cornelius Scipio Asiaticus"), the later conqueror of Antiochus, who had been sent off from Greece a few days before him.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-36)

#### Doubtful visit to Athens

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cato_the_Elder&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Doubtful visit to Athens")]

During the campaign in Greece under Glabrio, Plutarch's account (albeit rejected by historian Wilhelm Drumann) suggests that before the Battle of Thermopylae, Cato was chosen to prevent [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth "Ancient Corinth"), [Patrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrae "Patrae"), and [Aegium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegium "Aegium") from siding with Antiochus. During this period, Cato visited [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens "Athens") where, in trying to prevent the Athenians from listening to the propositions of the Seleucid king, Cato addressed them in a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") speech, which required an interpreter to be understood by the audience.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-37)

Whether this was out of necessity or merely a choice by Cato remains unclear, since the assertion that he might very well have already known Greek at the time can be made from anecdotal evidence. For example, Plutarch said that while at [Tarentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taranto "Taranto") in his youth he had developed a close friendship with Nearchus, who was himself a Greek [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher "Philosopher"). Similarly, [Aurelius Victor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelius_Victor "Aurelius Victor") stated he had received instruction in Greek from Ennius while praetor in Sardinia. Nevertheless, because his speech was an affair of state, it is probable that he complied with the Roman norms of the day in using the Latin language while practicing diplomacy, which was considered as a mark of Roman [dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignitas_(Roman_concept) "Dignitas (Roman concept)").[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-38)

His reputation as a soldier was now established; henceforth he preferred to serve the state at home, scrutinizing the conduct of the candidates for public honours and of generals in the field. If he was not personally engaged in the prosecution of the Scipiones (Africanus and [Asiaticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Asiaticus "Scipio Asiaticus")) for corruption, it was his spirit that animated the attack upon them. Even [Scipio Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Africanus "Scipio Africanus")—who refused to reply to the charge, saying only, "Romans, this is the day on which I conquered Hannibal" and was absolved by acclamation—found it necessary to retire, self-banished, to his villa at [Liternum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liternum "Liternum"). Cato's enmity dated from the African campaign when he quarrelled with Scipio for his lavish distribution of the spoil among the troops, and his general luxury and extravagance.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-39)

Cato was also opposed to the spread of Hellenic culture, which he believed threatened to destroy the rugged simplicity of the conventional Roman type. It was during this censorship that his determination to oppose Hellenism was most strongly exhibited, and hence, the behavior from which was derived the title (censor) by which he is most generally distinguished. He revised with unsparing severity the lists of senators and knights, ejecting from either order the men whom he judged unworthy of membership, either on moral grounds or on the basis of their lack of the prescribed means.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-40)

Senators were supposed to be independently wealthy, their income to be based on land ownership rather than commerce, and there was also a financial "means test." A Senator was expected to have what we would call a "net worth" of over a million sestertii, the standard Roman silver coin. Equivalencies in modern currencies are both misleading and impossible, but nevertheless, it would be fair to characterize the Roman Senate as a literal "Millionaires club."[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-41)[_[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please\_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_] The expulsion of [L. Quinctius Flamininus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Quinctius_Flamininus "L. Quinctius Flamininus") for wanton cruelty was an example of his rigid justice.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42)

His regulations against luxury were very stringent. He imposed a heavy tax upon dress and personal adornment, especially of women, and upon young slaves purchased as favourites. In 181 BC he supported the _lex Orchia_ (according to others, he first opposed its introduction, and subsequently its repeal), which prescribed a limit to the number of guests at an entertainment, and in 169 BC the _lex Voconia_, one of the provisions of which was intended to limit the accumulation of what Cato considered an undue amount of wealth in the hands of women.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42)

Among other things he repaired the [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_aqueduct "Roman aqueduct"), mended as well as extended the [sewage system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitary_sewer "Sanitary sewer"), and prevented private persons from drawing off public water for their own use. The [Aqua Appia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqua_Appia "Aqua Appia") was the first aqueduct of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"). It was constructed in 312 BC by Appius Claudius Caecus, the same Roman censor who also built the important Via Appia. Unauthorised plumbing into Rome's [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_(watercourse) "Aqueduct (watercourse)") had always been a problem, as [Frontinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontinus "Frontinus") records much later. Cato also ordered the demolition of shops and private houses which encroached on the public way, and built the first known [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica "Basilica") in 184 BC, named Basilica Porcia, in the [Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Forum "Roman Forum") near the [Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curia "Curia") (Livy, _History_, 39.44; Plutarch, _Marcus Cato_, 19).[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-Woolf2013-43) It served as a political and commercial activity center where courts were held and merchants accumulated. Some accounts state that the basilica was burned by the conflagration of [Publius Clodius Pulcher's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publius_Clodius_Pulcher "Publius Clodius Pulcher") funeral pyre after his death in 52 BC, and was probably never rebuilt.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-44)

Today, there are no remains of Basilica Porcia. Cato had also raised the amount paid by the [publicani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicani "Publicani") for the right to collect taxes and, at the same time, reduced the contract prices for the construction of public works.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42) Which was seen as most beneficial for the State and least for contractors, creating controversies around him. According to Plutarch, the Senate "strongly opposed the erection of the basilica". Cato's expenditure on public works were objected by the party of [Titus Flamininus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Quinctius_Flamininus "Titus Quinctius Flamininus") and also deemed trivial by the Senate. After gaining influence, Flamininus repealed the public rentals and contracts of Cato while encouraging tribunes to ferment opprobrium against him and fine him.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-45)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-46)

From the date of his censorship (184) to his death in 149, Cato held no public office, but continued to distinguish himself in the Senate as the persistent opponent of the new ideas. He was struck with horror, along with many other Romans, at the licence of the [Bacchanalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacchanalian "Bacchanalian") mysteries, which he attributed to the influence of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_civilization "Hellenistic civilization") manners, and he vehemently urged the dismissal of the philosophers [Carneades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carneades "Carneades"), [Diogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenes_of_Babylon "Diogenes of Babylon"), and [Critolaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critolaus "Critolaus"), who had come as ambassadors from [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens "Athens"), on account of what he believed was the dangerous nature of their ideas.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42) He also uttered warnings against the influence of Chaldean astrologers who had entered Italy along with Greek culture.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-47)

He had a horror of physicians, who were chiefly Greeks. He obtained the release of [Polybius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polybius "Polybius"), the historian, and his fellow prisoners, contemptuously asking whether the Senate had nothing more important to do than discuss whether a few Greeks should die at Rome or in their own land. It was not until his eightieth year that he made his first acquaintance with Greek literature,[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42) though some think after examining his writings that he may have had a knowledge of Greek works for much of his life.

In his last years, he was known for strenuously urging his countrymen to prosecute a [Third Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Punic_War "Third Punic War") and to destroy [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage"). In 157, he was one of the deputies sent to Carthage to arbitrate between the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginians "Carthaginians") and [Massinissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massinissa "Massinissa"), king of [Numidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia "Numidia"). The mission was unsuccessful and the commissioners returned home, but Cato was so struck by Carthage's growing prosperity that he was convinced that the security of Rome depended on its annihilation. From then on, he began concluding his speeches in the Senate—on any topic whatsoever—with the cry, "[Carthage must be destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthago_delenda_est "Carthago delenda est")" (_\_Carthago delenda est\__).[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-48) Other times, his phrase is fully quoted as "Moreover, I advise that Carthage must be destroyed" (_\_Ceterum censeo Carthaginem esse delendam\__).[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-49)[Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero "Cicero")'s [dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue_(fiction) "Dialogue (fiction)")_[Cato the Elder on Old Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De\_Senectute "De Senectute")_ also depicted Cato's antipathy to Carthage.[[n 4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-52) According to [Ben Kiernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Kiernan "Ben Kiernan"), Cato may have made the first recorded [incitement to genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incitement_to_genocide "Incitement to genocide").[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGordon201731%E2%80%9332-53)

To Cato the individual life was a continual discipline, and public life was the discipline of the many. He regarded the individual householder as the germ of the family, the family as the germ of the state. By strict economy of time he accomplished an immense amount of work; he demanded his dependents practice a similar dedication, and proved himself a hard husband, a strict father, and a severe and cruel master. There was little difference, apparently, in the esteem in which he held his wife and his slaves, although perhaps his pride caused him to take a warmer interest in his sons, [Marcus Porcius Cato Licinianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Porcius_Cato_Licinianus "Marcus Porcius Cato Licinianus") and [Marcus Porcius Cato Salonianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Porcius_Cato_Salonianus "Marcus Porcius Cato Salonianus").[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42)

To the Romans themselves little in this behavior seemed worthy of censure, it was respected rather as a traditional example of the old Roman manners. In the remarkable passage in which [Livy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livy "Livy") describes the character of Cato, there is no word of blame for the rigid discipline of his household.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-54)[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42)

[![Image 6](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Rustici_latini_volgarizzati.tif/lossy-page1-250px-Rustici_latini_volgarizzati.tif.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rustici_latini_volgarizzati.tif)

Italian translation of _De re rustica_ (1794)

Cato was and remains famous as an author as well. He was a historian, the first [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_literature "Latin literature") prose writer of any importance,[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-42) and the first author of a Roman history in Latin.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPelling1999-55) Some have argued that if it were not for the impact of Cato's writing, Latin might have been supplanted by Greek as the literary language of Rome. He was also one of the very few early Latin authors who could claim Latin as a native language.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-56)

*   His manual on running a farm (_[De agri cultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De\_agri\_cultura "De agri cultura")_ or "On Agriculture") (c.160) is his only work that survives complete. It is a miscellaneous collection of rules of husbandry and management, including sidelights on country life in the 2nd century BC. _De agri cultura_ was adopted by many as a textbook, at a time when Romans were expanding their agricultural activities into larger-scale and more specialized business ventures geared towards profitability. It assumes a farm run and staffed [by slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_ancient_Rome "Slavery in ancient Rome"): Cato advises hiring gangs for the olive harvest,[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-57) and was noted for his chilling advice on keeping slaves continually at work, on reducing rations for slaves when sick, and on selling slaves that are old or sickly.[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-58) Intended for reading aloud and discussing with farm workers, _De agri cultura_ was widely read and much quoted (sometimes inaccurately) by later Latin authors.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-59) Cato the Elder ranked the vineyard as the most important aspect when judging a farm. This was because of the profitability of the wine trade during that time. Grain pastures were ranked sixth due to the grain crisis.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-60)
*   The _[Origines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origines "Origines")_ in seven books (c. 168 BC)—of which several fragments still survive—related the history of the Italian towns with special attention to Rome, from their legendary or historical foundation to his own day. Written to teach Romans what it means to be Roman and used to teach his own son how to read, Cato the Elder wrote _ab urbe condita_ (from the founding of the city), and the early history is filled with legends illustrating Roman virtues. The _Origines_ also spoke of how not only Rome, but the other Italian towns were venerable, and claimed the Romans were indeed superior to the Greeks. As it avoided using [consular dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roman_consuls "List of Roman consuls"), it was not cited much by other historians.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-annals-61) It was a source for [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil "Virgil")'s _[Aeneid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeneid "Aeneid")_ and is referenced by other writers including [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero "Cicero").[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-62)
*   Under the Roman Empire a collection of about 150 political speeches by Cato existed. In these he pursued his political policies, fought verbal vendettas, and opposed what he saw as Rome's moral decline. Not even the titles of all of these speeches are now known, but fragments of some of them are preserved. Cato included parts of at least two of his speeches, _On Behalf of the Rhodians_ and _Against Galba_, in his historical work the _Origines_.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-63) The first to which we can give a date was _On the Improper Election of the Aediles_, delivered in 202 BC. The collection included several speeches from the year of his consulship, followed by a self-justifying retrospect _On His Consulship_ and by numerous speeches delivered when he was Censor. It is not clear whether Cato allowed others to read and copy his written texts (in other words, whether he "published" the speeches) or whether their circulation in written form began after his death.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-64)
*   _On Soldiery_ was perhaps a practical manual comparable to _On Farming_.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-65) This work is considered lost[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-66)
*   _On the Law Relating to Priests and Augurs_ was a topic that would follow naturally from some of the sections of _On Farming_. Only one brief extract from this work is known.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-67)
*   _Praecepta ad Filium_, "Maxims addressed to his son,"[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Elder#cite_note-68) from which the following extract survives:

> In due course, my son Marcus, I shall explain what I found out in Athens about these Greeks, and demonstrate what advantage there may be in looking into their writings (while not taking them too seriously). They are a worthless and unruly tribe. Take this as a prophecy: when those folk give us their writings they will corrupt everything. All the more if they send their doctors here. They have sworn to kill all barbarians with medicine—and they charge a fee for doing it, in order to be trusted and to work more easily. They call us barbarians, too, of course, and _[Opici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscans "Oscans")_, a dirtier name than the rest. I have forbidden you to deal with doctors.

*   A collection of _Sayings_, some of them translated from Greek.

The two surviving collections of proverbs known as the _[Distichs of Cato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distichs\_of\_Cato "Distichs of Cato")_ and the _[Monosticha Catonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Monosticha\_Catonis&action=edit&redlink=1 "Monosticha Catonis (page does not exist)")_ probably belong to the 4th century AD and are not works of Cato the Elder.

The [wrinkle ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrinkle_ridge "Wrinkle ridge") system [Dorsa Cato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsa_Cato "Dorsa Cato") on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") is named after Cato.

The [comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune "Comune") of [Monte Porzio Catone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Porzio_Catone "Monte Porzio Catone"), one of the [Castelli Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelli_Romani "Castelli Romani") and close to the ruins of Tusculum, is named in honour of the Porcius Cato family.

Cato is portrayed by [Vittorio Gassman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Gassman "Vittorio Gassman") in _[Scipione detto anche l'Africano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio\_the\_African "Scipio the African")_, a 1971 Italian film starring [Marcello Mastroianni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Mastroianni "Marcello Mastroianni") as Scipio Africanus.
