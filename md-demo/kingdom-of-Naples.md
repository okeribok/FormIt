The **Kingdom of Naples**,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-5) officially the **Kingdom of Sicily on this side of the lighthouse**,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-6) was a state that ruled the part of the [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula "Italian Peninsula") south of the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States "Papal States") between 1282 and 1816. It was established by the [War of the Sicilian Vespers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Sicilian_Vespers "War of the Sicilian Vespers") (1282–1302). Until then, the [island of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_Sicily "Island of Sicily") and [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy "Southern Italy") had constituted the "[Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily "Kingdom of Sicily")". When the island of Sicily revolted and was conquered by the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon"), it became a separate kingdom also called the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Trinacria "Kingdom of Trinacria").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-:0-7) This left the Neapolitan mainland in the possession of [Charles of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Anjou "Charles I of Anjou") who continued to use the name "Kingdom of Sicily". Later, two competing lines of the Angevin family competed for the Kingdom of Naples in the late 14th century, which resulted in the murder of [Joanna I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_I_of_Naples "Joanna I of Naples") at the hands of her successor, [Charles III of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Naples "Charles III of Naples"). Charles' daughter [Joanna II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_II_of_Naples "Joanna II of Naples") adopted King [Alfonso V of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_V_of_Aragon "Alfonso V of Aragon") as heir, who would then unite Naples into his [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon") dominions in 1442.

As part of the [Italian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars "Italian Wars"), France briefly ruled the territory in 1494 and at the beginning of the 16th century; it then [went to war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars_of_1499%E2%80%931504#Third_Italian_War "Italian Wars of 1499–1504") with Spain over the kingdom in 1502, a conflict that ended in a victory for [Ferdinand II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon "Ferdinand II of Aragon"), who was in full control of the kingdom by 1504. The Spanish held control of Naples throughout the 17th century where it remained an important source of economic and military power for the Spanish Crown. After the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession "War of the Spanish Succession") in the early 18th century, the possession of the kingdom again changed hands; the 1714 [Treaty of Rastatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rastatt "Treaty of Rastatt") saw Naples given to [Charles VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor") of the Austrian Habsburgs. However, Naples and Sicily were [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bitonto "Battle of Bitonto") by [Charles, Duke of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain "Charles III of Spain") (of the Spanish Bourbons) during the [War of the Polish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Polish_Succession "War of the Polish Succession") in 1734, he was then installed as King of Naples and Sicily from 1735. In 1816, Naples formally unified with the island of Sicily to form the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies "Kingdom of the Two Sicilies").

The Kingdom of Naples was one of the largest and most important [Italian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_states "Italian states") of its era. Its territory corresponded to the current Italian regions of [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania "Campania"), [Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabria "Calabria"), [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia "Apulia"), [Basilicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicata "Basilicata"), [Abruzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abruzzo "Abruzzo"), [Molise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise "Molise"), and also included some areas of today's southern and eastern [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio "Lazio").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-GalassoNapoli-8)

The term "Kingdom of Naples" is in near-universal use among historians, but it was not used officially by the government. Since the Angevins remained in power on the Italian peninsula, they kept the original name of the Kingdom of Sicily (_Regnum Siciliae_). At the end of the War of the Vespers, the [Peace of Caltabellotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Caltabellotta "Peace of Caltabellotta") (1302) provided that the name of the kingdom would be the Kingdom of Sicily _Citra Farum_. It had become known colloquially as the Kingdom of Naples (_Regnum Neapolitanum_ or _Regno di Napoli_).

In the late Middle Ages, it was common to distinguish the two Sicilies by noting its location relative to the rest of Italy and the _[Punta del Faro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta\_del\_Faro "Punta del Faro")_, i.e., the [Strait of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Messina "Strait of Messina"). The peninsular kingdom was known as Sicily _citra Farum_ or _al di qua del Faro_ ('on this side of Faro'), and the island kingdom was known as Sicily _ultra Farum_ or _di la del Faro_ (on the other side of Faro). When both kingdoms came under the rule of [Alfonso the Magnanimous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_the_Magnanimous "Alfonso the Magnanimous") in 1442, this usage became official, although [Ferdinand I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Naples "Ferdinand I of Naples") (1458–94) preferred the simple title King of Sicily (_Rex Sicilie_).[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-ES-9)

In the 18th century the Neapolitan intellectual [Giuseppe Maria Galanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Maria_Galanti "Giuseppe Maria Galanti") argued that Apulia was the true "national" name of the kingdom. By the time of Alfonso the Magnanimous, the two kingdoms were sufficiently distinct that they were no longer seen as divisions of a single kingdom. Despite being repeatedly in [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union "Personal union"), they remained administratively separate. In 1816, the two kingdoms finally merged to form the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-ES-9)

Naples, which was the capital of the [Duchy of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Naples "Duchy of Naples") since the 7th century, surrendered to [Roger II of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily "Roger II of Sicily") in 1137, and was annexed to the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily "Kingdom of Sicily").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-10) The Normans were the first to bring political unity to southern Italy in the centuries after the failure of the Byzantine effort to reconquer Italy. The Normans established a kingdom that included southern mainland Italy and the island of Sicily, which was primarily ruled from Palermo. The title of _King of Sicily_ was established by the [Antipope Anacletus II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipope_Anacletus_II "Antipope Anacletus II") as early as 1130 and subsequently legitimized, in 1139, by [Pope Innocent II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_II "Pope Innocent II"). Since the royal titles over the State had been assigned to the Normans by Innocent II, the popes, in particular [Pope Innocent III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III "Pope Innocent III") and [Pope Innocent IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV "Pope Innocent IV"), claimed the feudal rights of the [Church State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States "Papal States") over the Kingdom.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-11) After [Constance, Queen of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_I_of_Sicily "Constance I of Sicily") married [Henry VI, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Henry VI, Holy Roman Emperor"), the region was inherited by their son [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor"), as King of Sicily. The region that later became the separate Kingdom of Naples under the Angevins formed part of the Kingdom of Sicily, which included the island of Sicily and [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia "Apulia").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-:0-7)

Following the rebellion in 1282, King [Charles I of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Sicily "Charles I of Sicily") (Charles of Anjou) was forced to leave the island of [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily") by [Peter III of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_III_of_Aragon "Peter III of Aragon")'s troops. Charles, however, maintained his possessions on the mainland, customarily known as the "Kingdom of [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples")", after its capital city.

Charles and his [Angevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou "Capetian House of Anjou") successors maintained a claim to Sicily, warring against the [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Aragonese_monarchs "List of Aragonese monarchs") until 1373, when Queen [Joan I of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_I_of_Naples "Joan I of Naples") formally renounced the claim by the [Treaty of Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Villeneuve "Treaty of Villeneuve"). Joan's reign was contested by [Louis the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_the_Great "Louis the Great"), the Angevin King of [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hungary "History of Hungary"), who [captured the kingdom several times (1348–1352)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_campaigns_of_Louis_the_Great "Neapolitan campaigns of Louis the Great").

Queen Joan I also played a part in the ultimate demise of the first Kingdom of Naples. As she was childless, she adopted [Louis I, Duke of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_I,_Duke_of_Anjou "Louis I, Duke of Anjou"), as her heir, in spite of the claims of her cousin, the Prince of Durazzo, effectively setting up a junior Angevin line in competition with the senior line. This led to Joan I's murder at the hands of the Prince of Durazzo in 1382, and his seizing of the throne as [Charles III of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Naples "Charles III of Naples").

The two competing Angevin lines contested each other for the possession of the Kingdom of Naples over the following decades. In 1389 [Louis II of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_II_of_Anjou "Louis II of Anjou") son of Louis I managed to seize the throne from [Ladislas of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladislas_of_Naples "Ladislas of Naples") son of Charles III, but was expelled by Ladislas in 1399. Charles III's daughter [Joanna II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_II_of_Naples "Joanna II of Naples") (r. 1414–1435) adopted [Alfonso V of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_V_of_Aragon "Alfonso V of Aragon") (whom she later repudiated) and [Louis III of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_III_of_Anjou "Louis III of Anjou") as heirs alternately, finally settling succession on Louis' brother [René of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_of_Anjou "René of Anjou") of the junior Angevin line, and he succeeded her in 1435.

René of Anjou temporarily united the claims of junior and senior Angevin lines.

In 1442, [Alfonso V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_V_of_Aragon "Alfonso V of Aragon")[conquered the Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragonese_conquest_of_Naples "Aragonese conquest of Naples") and unified Sicily and Naples once again as dependencies of [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon"). At his death in 1458, the [War of the Neapolitan Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Naples#The_Angevin-Aragonese_War_(1460%E2%80%931464) "Ferdinand I of Naples") (1458–1462) erupted, after which the kingdom was again separated and Naples was inherited by [Ferdinand I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Naples "Ferdinand I of Naples"), Alfonso's illegitimate son.

When Ferdinand I died in 1494, [Charles VIII of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VIII_of_France "Charles VIII of France") invaded Italy, using as a pretext the Angevin claim to the throne of Naples, which his father had inherited in 1481 on the will of [Charles IV of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IV_of_Anjou "Charles IV of Anjou"), nephew and heir of King René who had no surviving son. This began the [Italian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars "Italian Wars").

### From French rule to Spanish viceroyalty

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Naples&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: From French rule to Spanish viceroyalty")]

Charles VIII expelled [Alfonso II of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_II_of_Naples "Alfonso II of Naples") from Naples in 1495, but was soon forced to withdraw due to the support of [Ferdinand II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon "Ferdinand II of Aragon") for his cousin, Alfonso II's son [Ferrantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Naples "Ferdinand II of Naples"). Ferrantino was restored to the throne but died in 1496 and was succeeded by his uncle, [Frederick IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_IV_of_Naples "Frederick IV of Naples").

Charles VIII's successor, [Louis XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XII "Louis XII") reiterated the French claim. In 1501, he occupied Naples and partitioned the kingdom with Ferdinand of Aragon, who abandoned his cousin King Frederick. However, disputes over ownership of key Neapolitan territories made the deal quickly fall through, and Aragon and France [resumed their war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Wars_of_1499%E2%80%931504#Third_Italian_War "Italian Wars of 1499–1504") over the kingdom in 1502. The Spanish troops occupying [Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabria "Calabria") and [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia "Apulia"), led by [Gonzalo Fernandez de Cordova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Fernandez_de_Cordova "Gonzalo Fernandez de Cordova"), invaded and expelled all Frenchmen from the area. The resulting Aragonese victory left Ferdinand in full control of the kingdom by 1504 and Naples became a constituent kingdom of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon "Crown of Aragon").

The peace treaties that continued were never definitive, but they established at least that the title of [King of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Naples "King of Naples") was reserved for Ferdinand's grandson, the future [Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor"). Ferdinand nevertheless continued in possession of the kingdom, being considered the legitimate heir of his uncle Alfonso I of Naples (Alfonso V of Aragon) and also to the former [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily "Kingdom of Sicily") (_Regnum Utriusque Siciliae_).

Naples during the revolt of [Masaniello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaniello "Masaniello") in 1647

The kingdom continued to be disputed between France and Spain for the next several decades, but French efforts to gain control of it became feebler as [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain "Habsburg Spain") power grew, and never genuinely endangered Spanish control.

In 1544, [Algerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_of_Algiers "Regency of Algiers")[corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_corsairs "Barbary corsairs") sailed into the Bay of Naples and [raided it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_on_the_Bay_of_Naples_(1544) "Raid on the Bay of Naples (1544)"). They then took an estimated 7,000 Neapolitan [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade "Barbary slave trade").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-12)

The French finally abandoned their claims to Naples by the [Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Cateau-Cambr%C3%A9sis "Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis") in 1559. In the Treaty of London (1557), five cities on the coast of [Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany "Tuscany") were designated the _Stato dei Presidi_ ([State of the Presidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Presidi "State of the Presidi")), and part of the Kingdom of Naples.

As the most populous holding of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire "Spanish Empire") outside of [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile "Kingdom of Castile") itself (with 3 million inhabitants in 1600),[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-13) Naples remained an important source of economic and military power for the Spanish. Heavy taxation was levied upon the kingdom to pay for Spain's wars, especially after 1580. Beyond dispatching troops to fight the [Eighty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighty_Years%27_War "Eighty Years' War") in the Low Countries, Naples also disbursed a third of the military expenditures accruing to the Spanish controlled [Duchy of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan "Duchy of Milan") and paid for the Spanish garrisons in Tuscany. This cost the kingdom 800,000 ducats annually, or about a third of the kingdom's revenues; moreover, the public debt also had a military origin, and interest payments on it devoured 40 percent of all tax income. Naples was rich enough to redeem the debt and pay an attractive ten percent in full to lenders. While the soldiers of Naples were under the command of the Spanish viceroy, Neapolitan nobles enjoyed ascendancy in the assemblies and committees that financed and administered the army.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-14)

The kingdom suffered a heavy burden from the [Franco-Spanish War (1635–1659)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Spanish_War_(1635%E2%80%931659) "Franco-Spanish War (1635–1659)"). From 1631 to 1636 alone, Naples sent 53,500 soldiers and 3.5 million [scudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_scudo "Italian scudo") to support the Spanish king.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-15) This was actually more than was raised in the same time by [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile "Crown of Castile"), which had a population twice the size. Naples provided and paid for 10,000 troops and 1,000 horses annually from 1630 to 1643, on top of a 1 million ducat annual subsidy for the war effort and more funds and soldiers for the kingdom's garrisons and navy.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-16) The kingdom was increasingly forced to revert to borrowing to finance the war as it went on, which it could do due to its good credit. From 1612 to 1646, Neapolitan taxes tripled and the public debt quintupled, and 57 percent of the kingdom's revenue was devoted to interest payments. Spain's wars crushed the Neapolitan economy. Furthermore, 90 percent of taxes were collected by state creditors, meaning the state paid an effective interest rate of 70 percent annually on the money it borrowed to fight the war. The kingdom started selling state assets to anyone willing to buy them, which usually ended up being barons; these assets included prisons, forests, buildings, and even royal fortresses, and titles.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-17)

Due to this excessive taxation the people of Naples rose in revolt in 1647, forming the [Neapolitan Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Republic_(1647) "Neapolitan Republic (1647)") with French assistance. The revolt was suppressed later that year by Spanish troops.

### Austrian Habsburgs and Bourbons

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Naples&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Austrian Habsburgs and Bourbons")]

[![Image 14](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Province_napoletane_%281454%29.PNG/250px-Province_napoletane_%281454%29.PNG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Province_napoletane_(1454).PNG)

Provinces

After the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession "War of the Spanish Succession") in the early 18th century, possession of the kingdom again changed hands. Under the terms of the [Treaty of Rastatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rastatt "Treaty of Rastatt") in 1714, Naples was given to [Charles VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor"), the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor "Holy Roman Emperor"). He also gained control of [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily") in 1720, but Austrian rule did not last long. Both Naples and Sicily were [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bitonto "Battle of Bitonto") by a Spanish army during the [War of the Polish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Polish_Succession "War of the Polish Succession") in 1734, and [Charles, Duke of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain "Charles III of Spain"), a younger son of King [Philip V of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Spain "Philip V of Spain"), the first member of the French [House of Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon "House of Bourbon") to rule in Spain, was installed as King of Naples and Sicily from 1735. When Charles inherited the Spanish throne from his older half-brother in 1759, he left Naples and Sicily to his younger son, [Ferdinand IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies "Ferdinand I of the Two Sicilies"). Despite the two Kingdoms being in a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union "Personal union") under the Habsburg and Bourbon dynasties, they remained constitutionally separate.

Being a member of the [House of Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon "House of Bourbon"), Ferdinand IV was a natural opponent of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution") and [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon"). On 29 November 1798, he effectively started the [War of the Second Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Second_Coalition "War of the Second Coalition") by briefly occupying Rome, but was expelled from it by French Revolutionary forces within the year and safely returned home. Soon afterwards, on 23 December 1798, Ferdinand fled [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples") to [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo "Palermo") as a French army closed in. In January 1799, the French armies installed a [Parthenopaean Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenopaean_Republic "Parthenopaean Republic"), but this proved short-lived, and a peasant counter-revolution inspired by the clergy allowed Ferdinand to return to his capital. However, in 1801 Ferdinand was compelled to make important concessions to the French by the [Treaty of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Florence "Treaty of Florence"), which reinforced France's position as the dominant power in mainland Italy.

Territorial evolution

Ferdinand's decision to ally with the [Third Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Coalition "Third Coalition") against [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon") in 1805 proved more damaging. In 1806, following decisive victories over the allied armies at [Austerlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Austerlitz "Battle of Austerlitz") and over the Neapolitans at [Campo Tenese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Campo_Tenese "Battle of Campo Tenese"), Napoleon installed his brother, [Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte "Joseph Bonaparte") as King of Naples, he conferred the title "Prince of Naples" to be hereditary on his children and grandchildren. When Joseph was sent off to [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_under_Joseph_Bonaparte "Spain under Joseph Bonaparte") two years later, he was replaced by Napoleon's sister [Caroline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Bonaparte "Caroline Bonaparte") and his brother-in-law Marshal [Joachim Murat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Murat "Joachim Murat").

Meanwhile, Ferdinand had fled to Sicily, where he retained his throne, despite successive attempts by Murat to invade the island. The British would defend Sicily for the remainder of the war but despite the Kingdom of Sicily nominally being part of the [Fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Coalition "Fourth Coalition"), [Fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Coalition "Fifth Coalition"), and [Sixth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Coalition "Sixth Coalition") Coalitions against Napoleon, Ferdinand and the British were unable to ever challenge French control of the Italian mainland.

After Napoleon's defeat in 1814, Murat reached an agreement with Austria and was allowed to retain the throne of Naples, despite the lobbying efforts of Ferdinand and his supporters. However, with most of the other powers, particularly Britain, hostile towards him and dependent on the uncertain support of Austria, Murat's position became less and less secure. Therefore, when Napoleon returned to France for the [Hundred Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days "Hundred Days") in 1815, Murat once again sided with him. Realising the Austrians would soon attempt to remove him, Murat gave the [Rimini Proclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini_Proclamation "Rimini Proclamation") hoping to save his kingdom by allying himself with Italian nationalists.

The ensuing [Neapolitan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_War "Neapolitan War") between Murat and the Austrians was short, ending with a decisive victory for the Austrian forces at the [Battle of Tolentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tolentino "Battle of Tolentino"). Murat was forced to flee, and Ferdinand IV was restored to the throne of Naples. Murat would attempt to regain his throne but was quickly captured and executed by firing squad in [Pizzo, Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzo,_Calabria "Pizzo, Calabria"). The next year, 1816, finally saw the formal union of the Kingdom of Naples with the Kingdom of Sicily into the new [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies "Kingdom of the Two Sicilies").

The languages of the Kingdom of Naples reflect the rich cultural and historical diversity of this southern Italian state. The official language was Italian, used for bureaucracy, education, and formal communications. However, the languages spoken daily by the population were varied. In the mainland territories, such as Campania and Calabria, the [Neapolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_language "Neapolitan language") and [Calabrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Calabria#Calabrian_(calabrese) "Languages of Calabria") dialects predominated, which are part of the group of intermediate southern languages.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-18)

In Sicily, the main language spoken was [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language "Sicilian language"), a language with its own history and literature. Furthermore, in the various territories of the kingdom, there were linguistic minorities, such as the [Greek speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabrian_Greek "Calabrian Greek") in Calabria and Puglia, and the [Albanian speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arb%C3%ABresh_language "Arbëresh language") in Sicily and Calabria, who spoke Greek and Albanian respectively. This linguistic plurality was a reflection of the history of colonization and domination of different cultures that characterized the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-19)

As often happens, the evolution of the various dialects of the kingdom is not easily traceable, since the dialects were practically never transcribed and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language "Latin language") and [Italian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language "Italian language") were used for written communication. In the kingdom, written communication was itself very rare considering the high rate of illiteracy. Nevertheless, traces of dialects are sometimes found in some expressions used by educated people, for example in correspondence; also some methodologies such as [comparative linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_linguistics "Comparative linguistics") enables to trace some features of the evolution of dialects.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-ReferenceA-20)

One of the few testimonies relating to the language in use in the Middle Ages in the Kingdom of Naples is that of [Salimbene de Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salimbene_de_Adam "Salimbene de Adam") (13th century AD) who, in his _Chronicle_, notes the total absence of the use of courtesy pronouns in the languages of the Apulians and Sicilians who, according to what the author reports, the courtesy pronouns were almost absent and they would use the _tu_ ("you") even to refer to the emperor while, on the contrary, the Lombards would use the pronoun _voi_ (courtesy pronoun) even for referring to a child. These regional differences also occur in contemporary Italian.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-ReferenceA-20)

Religions in the Kingdom of Naples and, later, the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies "Kingdom of the Two Sicilies") were mainly [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism "Roman Catholicism"), which was the state religion and deeply intertwined with the political, social and cultural life of the kingdom. The Catholic Church had a strong influence and ran many educational and charitable institutions. Despite the predominance of Catholicism, other religious communities were also present and small [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Jews "Italian Jews") communities lived mainly in the major cities such as [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples") and [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo "Palermo"), where they enjoyed relative tolerance and actively participated in economic life. In addition, there were minorities of [Greek-Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Albanian_Catholic_Church "Italo-Albanian Catholic Church") rite, especially in Calabria and Sicily, due to the [historical Byzantine presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catepanate_of_Italy "Catepanate of Italy") and the immigration of [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_Italy "Albanians in Italy") communities. These communities maintained their religious and [cultural traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Rite "Byzantine Rite"), while remaining in communion with the Church of Rome.

Much more varied, however, was the amount of religions in the Kingdom of Naples during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages"). Most of the cities and towns had Jewish as well as [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Italy "Islam in Italy") communities (the so-called _i mori_); those communities significantly decreased in the following centuries. In particular, the Jewish communities were significantly reduced after the expulsion edicts issued during the 16th century by [Ferdinand the Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_the_Catholic "Ferdinand the Catholic") (1510) and other kings. With the edict of expulsion of [Charles V of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V_of_Habsburg "Charles V of Habsburg") (1541), Jews and new converts became mostly insignificant in the Kingdom of Naples.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-21)

### Customs and traditions

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Naples&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Customs and traditions")]

[![Image 17](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8e/Country_festival_near_Pozzuoli_by_Franz_Ludwig_Catel.jpg/250px-Country_festival_near_Pozzuoli_by_Franz_Ludwig_Catel.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Country_festival_near_Pozzuoli_by_Franz_Ludwig_Catel.jpg)

Festival near [Pozzuoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozzuoli "Pozzuoli"), by [Franz Ludwig Catel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Ludwig_Catel "Franz Ludwig Catel")

The [Neapolitan Republic of 1647](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Republic_(1647) "Neapolitan Republic (1647)") greatly influenced subsequent historical development of revolutions in Europe over the 17th century and 18th century, and it even inspired the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution") of 1789. It is noteworthy what was then reported by travellers (British for the most part) in the 18th century and 19th century inside [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples"); they reported, in the [daguerreotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daguerreotypes "Daguerreotypes") and in the pictorial representations of their travels, that spaghetti were eaten using bare hands in the streets of Naples. This tradition soon became a proper tourist attraction, so that tourists gave small change to the _[lazzaroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazzaroni\_(Naples) "Lazzaroni (Naples)")_ who then rushed to buy spaghetti and then eat them with their own hands.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-22)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-23)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-24)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-25)

This custom was preserved well beyond the period of existence of the Kingdom of Naples and until very recent times; it was also the subject of popular scenes in a few films of the 20th century and, in particular, in the movie _[Poverty and Nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty\_and\_Nobility "Poverty and Nobility")_ (1954) starring the Italian actor [Totò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tot%C3%B2 "Totò").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-26)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-27)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-28) Other distinctive characteristics of the inhabitants of the kingdom were the low level of literacy and widespread selfishness.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-29) In this context, there were regions such as [Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabria "Calabria") whose inhabitants were referred to by Europeans as the last "European savages".[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-30)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-31)[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-32)

For a few centuries, [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples") has often been described as "a paradise inhabited by devils" (in Italian: _un paradiso abitato da diavoli_). Italian historian [Benedetto Croce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedetto_Croce "Benedetto Croce"), in one of his essays, traced the origin of this saying and he discovered its first occurrence in a letter written in 1539 by Bernardino Daniello and sent to Alessandro Corvino. In 1592, it occurred for the first time outside the Italian peninsula in a letter by Sir [Henry Wotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wotton "Henry Wotton"). As pointed out by Benedetto Croce, this saying was used over the centuries to denote different things, i.e. in some cases Naples was described as a city of sin and vices, most notably prostitution. In other cases, it was used to denote the rudeness, mischievousness and lack of intelligence of its population (for example, some statements of the book _Le facezie del Pievano Arlotto_ on [Piovano Arlotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piovano_Arlotto "Piovano Arlotto")) – 14th century). Neapolitans were also described as extremely jealous and "tyrants of women".[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-33)[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-34)[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-35) In 1707, the German author Johann Andreas Bühel published a whole essay on this saying, i.e. _Regnum Neapolitanum Paradisus Est, Sed A Diabolis Habitatus_.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_note-36)

2.   **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples#cite_ref-3)**Presumed motto, not official. A legend has it that the motto of the kingdom was _Noxias herbas_ ('noxious herbs'), chosen by [Charles I of Anjou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Anjou "Charles I of Anjou") in reference to the [rake](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rake_(heraldry)&action=edit&redlink=1 "Rake (heraldry) (page does not exist)")[[it](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastrello_(araldica) "it:Rastrello (araldica)")] present on the coat of arms, which would have symbolized the expulsion of the Swabian "weed".
