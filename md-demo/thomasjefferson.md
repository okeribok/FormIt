**Thomas Jefferson** (April 13 [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates") April 2], 1743 – July 4, 1826) was a [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States "Founding Fathers of the United States") and the third [president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") from 1801 to 1809. He was the second [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States "Vice President of the United States") under [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"). Jefferson was the primary author of the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence"), and a leading proponent of [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy"), [republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism "Republicanism") and [natural rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_rights "Natural rights").

Jefferson was born into the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia "Colony of Virginia")'s [planter class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planter_class "Planter class"). During the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution"), he represented Virginia in the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress "Second Continental Congress"), which unanimously adopted the Declaration of Independence. Jefferson's advocacy for [individual rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_and_group_rights "Individual and group rights"), including [freedom of thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_thought "Freedom of thought"), [speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_speech "Freedom of speech"), and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion "Freedom of religion"), helped shape the ideological foundations of the revolution.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-4)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-5) Jefferson served as the second [governor of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Virginia "Governor of Virginia") from 1779 to 1781. In 1785, [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Confederation "Congress of the Confederation") appointed him as [U.S. Minister to France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_France "List of ambassadors of the United States to France"), where he served from 1785 to 1789. President [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington") then appointed Jefferson the nation's first secretary of state, where he served from 1790 to 1793. In 1792, Jefferson and political ally [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison "James Madison") organized the [Democratic-Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-Republican_Party "Democratic-Republican Party") to oppose the [Federalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_Party "Federalist Party") during the formation of the nation's [First Party System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Party_System "First Party System"). Jefferson and Federalist [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") became both personal friends and political rivals. In the [1796 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1796_United_States_presidential_election "1796 United States presidential election") between the two, Jefferson came in second, which made him Adams' vice president under the electoral laws of the time. Four years later, in the [1800 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_United_States_presidential_election "1800 United States presidential election"), Jefferson again challenged Adams and won the presidency. When running for reelection in [1804](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1804_United_States_presidential_election "1804 United States presidential election"), Jefferson overwhelmingly defeated the Federalists' [Charles Cotesworth Pinckney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Cotesworth_Pinckney "Charles Cotesworth Pinckney") of South Carolina.

[Jefferson's presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Thomas_Jefferson "Presidency of Thomas Jefferson") assertively defended the nation's shipping and trade interests against [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_corsairs "Barbary corsairs") and aggressive British trade policies, promoted a western expansionist policy with the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase"), which doubled the nation's geographic size, and reduced military forces and expenditures following successful negotiations with [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France "Kingdom of France"). In his second presidential term, Jefferson was beset by difficulties at home, including [the trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burr_conspiracy "Burr conspiracy") of his former vice president [Aaron Burr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Burr "Aaron Burr"). In 1807, Jefferson implemented the [Embargo Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo_Act_of_1807 "Embargo Act of 1807") to defend the nation's industries from British threats to U.S. shipping, limit foreign trade, and stimulate the birth of the [American manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_the_United_States "Manufacturing in the United States").

Jefferson is [ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_rankings_of_presidents_of_the_United_States "Historical rankings of presidents of the United States") among the upper tier of U.S. presidents both by scholars and in public opinion. Presidential scholars and historians have praised Jefferson's advocacy of religious freedom and tolerance, his peaceful acquisition of the [Louisiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Territory "Louisiana Territory") from France, and his leadership in supporting the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition "Lewis and Clark Expedition"). They acknowledge his lifelong ownership of large numbers of slaves, but offer varying interpretations of his [views on and relationship with slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_and_slavery "Thomas Jefferson and slavery").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cogliano-2008-6)

## Early life and education

[![Image 3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/ChristopherWrenBuilding.jpg/250px-ChristopherWrenBuilding.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ChristopherWrenBuilding.jpg)

The [Wren Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wren_Building "Wren Building") at the [College of William & Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_William_%26_Mary "College of William & Mary") in [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Virginia "Williamsburg, Virginia"), where Jefferson studied in 1761 and 1762

Jefferson was born on April 13, 1743 (April 2, 1743, [Old Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates"), [Julian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar "Julian calendar")), at the family's [Shadwell Plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadwell,_Virginia "Shadwell, Virginia") in the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia "Colony of Virginia"), then one of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies "Thirteen Colonies") of [British America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America "British America"). He was the third of ten children.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-7) His father, [Peter Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Jefferson "Peter Jefferson"), was a planter and surveyor; his mother was [Jane Randolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Randolph_Jefferson "Jane Randolph Jefferson").[[b]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-10) Peter Jefferson moved his family to [Tuckahoe Plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuckahoe_(plantation) "Tuckahoe (plantation)") in 1745 following the death of [William Randolph III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Randolph_III_(son_of_Thomas) "William Randolph III (son of Thomas)"), the plantation's owner and Jefferson's friend, who in his will had named Peter guardian of Randolph's children. The Jeffersons returned to Shadwell before October 1753.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-11)

Jefferson began his education together with the [Randolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_family_of_Virginia "Randolph family of Virginia") children at Tuckahoe under tutors.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-12) Thomas' father Peter, who was self-taught and regretted not having a formal education, entered Thomas into an English school at age five. In 1752, at age nine, he attended a local school run by a [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism "Presbyterianism") minister and also began studying the natural world, which he grew to love. He studied [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language"), and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"), and began learning to ride horses. Thomas read books from his father's modest library.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-bowers12-13) He was taught from 1758 to 1760 by the Reverend [James Maury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Maury "James Maury") near [Gordonsville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordonsville,_Virginia "Gordonsville, Virginia"), where he studied history, science, and the [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics "Classics") while boarding with Maury's family.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-bowers12-13)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-14) Jefferson came to know various Native Americans including [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee "Cherokee") chief [Ostenaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostenaco "Ostenaco"), who often stopped at Shadwell to visit on their way to [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Virginia "Williamsburg, Virginia") to trade.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-15)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-16) In Williamsburg, the young Jefferson met and came to admire [Patrick Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Henry "Patrick Henry").[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-17)

Thomas's father died in 1757, and his estate was divided between his sons, Thomas and [Randolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_Jefferson "Randolph Jefferson").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-18)[John Harvie Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harvie_Sr. "John Harvie Sr.") became 14-year-old Thomas' guardian.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Woods-19) Thomas inherited approximately 5,000 acres (7.8 sq mi; 20.2 km 2), which included the land on which he later built [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello") in 1772.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Malone437-20)

In 1761, at the age of seventeen, Jefferson entered the [College of William & Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_William_%26_Mary "College of William & Mary") in Williamsburg, where he studied mathematics and philosophy with [William Small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Small "William Small"). Under Small's tutelage, Jefferson encountered the ideas of British [empiricists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism "Empiricism"), including [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke"), [Francis Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Bacon "Francis Bacon"), and [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton"). Small also introduced Jefferson to [George Wythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Wythe "George Wythe") and [Francis Fauquier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Fauquier "Francis Fauquier"). Small, Wythe, and Fauquier recognized Jefferson as a man of exceptional ability and included him in their inner circle, where he became a regular member of their Friday dinner parties. Jefferson later wrote that, while there, he "heard more common good sense, more rational & philosophical conversations than in all the rest of my life".[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-21)

During his first year in college, Jefferson spent considerable time attending parties and dancing and was not very frugal with his expenditures; in his second year, regretting that he had squandered away time and money in his first year, he committed to studying fifteen hours a day.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-22) While at William & Mary, Jefferson became a member of the [Flat Hat Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_Hat_Club "Flat Hat Club"), the nation's oldest secret society, a small group whose members included St. George Tucker, Edmund Randolph, and James Innes.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-23)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-24)

Jefferson concluded his formal studies in April 1762.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-25) He [read the law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_law "Reading law") under Wythe's tutelage while working as a [law clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_clerk "Law clerk") in his office.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-26) Jefferson was well-read in a broad variety of subjects, including law, philosophy, history, natural law, natural religion, ethics,[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] and several areas of science, including astronomy[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-27) and agriculture.

Jefferson kept two [commonplace books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonplace_book "Commonplace book"): from about age 15 to 30, he compiled a book of sayings and quotations, published in the 20th century as _Jefferson's Literary Commonplace Book_.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-28) During his years of legal study under Wythe, Jefferson began recording his notes on law, history, and philosophy, and continued to do so until the end of his life; his _Legal Commonplace Book_ was also published in the 20th century.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-29)

On July 20, 1765, Jefferson's sister Martha married his close friend and college companion [Dabney Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabney_Carr_(Virginia_assemblyman) "Dabney Carr (Virginia assemblyman)"), which greatly pleased Jefferson. In October of that year, however, Jefferson's sister Jane unexpectedly died at age 25. Her death plunged Jefferson into a months-long period of mourning that was spoken of amongst the family.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-30)

Jefferson treasured his books and amassed three sizable libraries in his lifetime. He began assembling his first library, which grew to 200 volumes, in his youth.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-31) Wythe was so impressed with Jefferson that he later bequeathed his entire library to him.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-32) In 1770, however, Jefferson's first library was destroyed in a fire at his Shadwell home. His second library, which replenished the first, grew to nearly 6,500 volumes by 1814.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-33) Jefferson organized his books into three broad categories of the human mind: memory, reason, and imagination.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-34) After [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland") forces set the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress") on fire in the [Burning of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_of_Washington "Burning of Washington") in 1814, Jefferson sold his second library to the [U.S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States") for $23,950, hoping to help jumpstart the Library of Congress's rebuilding. Jefferson used a portion of the proceeds to pay off some of his large debt. Jefferson soon resumed collecting his third personal library. In a letter to [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"), Jefferson wrote, "I cannot live without books."[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-library-35)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-36) By the time of Jefferson's death a decade later, his third and final library had grown to nearly 2,000 volumes.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-37)

## Career

### Lawyer and House of Burgesses

In 1767, Jefferson was granted admission to the Virginia bar, and lived with his mother at Shadwell.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-38) Between 1769 and 1775, he represented [Albemarle County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albemarle_County,_Virginia "Albemarle County, Virginia") in Virginia's [House of Burgesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Burgesses "House of Burgesses").[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-39) While serving in the House of Burgesses, Jefferson pursued reforms to slavery, including writing and sponsoring legislation in 1769 to strip power from the royal governor and courts, instead providing masters of slaves with the discretion to emancipate them. Jefferson persuaded his cousin [Richard Bland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bland "Richard Bland") to spearhead the legislation's passage, but it faced strong opposition in a state whose economy was largely agrarian.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-40)

As a lawyer, Jefferson took on seven freedom-seeking enslaved people as clients[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-41) and [waived his fee for one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_bono "Pro bono") he claimed should be freed before the minimum statutory age for emancipation.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Gordon99-42) Jefferson invoked [natural law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law "Natural law"), arguing "everyone comes into the world with a right to his own person and using it at his own will... This is what is called personal liberty, and is given him by the author of nature, because it is necessary for his own sustenance." The judge cut him off and ruled against his client. As a consolation, Jefferson gave his client some money, which was conceivably used to aid his escape shortly thereafter.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Gordon99-42) Jefferson's underlying intellectual argument that all people were entitled by their creator to what he labeled a "natural right" to liberty is a theme that he later prominently incorporated into the Declaration of Independence.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-43) In 1767, Jefferson took on 68 cases for the General Court of Virginia and was counsel in three notable cases of that era, _Howell v. Netherland_ (1770), _Bolling v. Bolling_ (1771), and _Blair v. Blair_ (1772).[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Konig_Time_Line-44)

In 1774, Jefferson authored a resolution calling for a boycott of all British goods in protest of the [British Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain "Parliament of Great Britain")'s passing of the [Intolerable Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intolerable_Acts "Intolerable Acts"). Jefferson's resolution was later expanded into _[A Summary View of the Rights of British America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A\_Summary\_View\_of\_the\_Rights\_of\_British\_America "A Summary View of the Rights of British America")_, published that year in which he argued that people have the right to [govern themselves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governing_colony "Self-governing colony").[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-45)

### Monticello, marriage, and family

[![Image 4: Monticello plantation house](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Monticello_2010-10-29.jpg/250px-Monticello_2010-10-29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Monticello_2010-10-29.jpg)

[Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello"), Jefferson's home near [Charlottesville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia "Charlottesville, Virginia")

In 1768, Jefferson began constructing his primary residence, [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello") near present-day [Charlottesville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia "Charlottesville, Virginia"). Its [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language "Italian language") name means "Little Mountain" in English. Monticello is located on a hilltop overlooking his 5,000-acre (20 km 2; 7.8 mi 2) plantation.[[c]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-47) He spent most of his adult life designing Monticello as an architect and was quoted as saying, "Architecture is my delight, and putting up, and pulling down, one of my favorite amusements."[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-48) Construction was done mostly by local masons and carpenters, assisted by Jefferson's slaves.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-49) He moved into the South Pavilion in 1770. Turning Monticello into a [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture "Neoclassical architecture") masterpiece in the [Palladian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladian_architecture "Palladian architecture") style became Jefferson's lifelong project.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-50)

On January 1, 1772, Jefferson married his third cousin,[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-51)[Martha Wayles Skelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Jefferson "Martha Jefferson"), a 23-year-old widow of Bathurst Skelton.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Tucker_p.47-52)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-53) She was a frequent hostess for Jefferson and managed the large household. Historian [Dumas Malone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumas_Malone "Dumas Malone") described the marriage as the happiest period of Jefferson's life.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-54) Martha was a skilled pianist; Jefferson often accompanied her on the violin or cello.[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-55) During their ten-year marriage, Martha bore six children: [Martha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Jefferson_Randolph "Martha Jefferson Randolph") "Patsy" (1772–1836); Jane Randolph (1774–1775); an unnamed son who lived for only a few weeks in 1777; [Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Jefferson_Eppes "Mary Jefferson Eppes") "Polly" (1778–1804); Lucy Elizabeth (1780–1781); and another Lucy Elizabeth (1782–1784).[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-56)[[d]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-59) Only Martha and Mary survived to adulthood.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Martha-60) Martha's father, John Wayles, died in 1773, and the couple inherited 135 enslaved people, 11,000 acres (45 km 2; 17 mi 2), and the estate's debts. The debts took Jefferson years to satisfy, contributing to his financial problems.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Tucker_p.47-52)

Martha later suffered from ill health, including [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes "Diabetes"), and frequent childbirth weakened her. A few months after the birth of her last child, she died on September 6, 1782, with Jefferson at her bedside. Shortly before her death, Martha made Jefferson promise never to marry again, telling him that she could not bear to have another mother raise her children.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-61) Jefferson was grief-stricken by her death, relentlessly pacing back and forth for roughly three weeks, and finally emerging to take long rambling rides on secluded roads with his daughter, Martha, who said she was "a solitary witness to many a violent burst of grief".[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Martha-60)[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Halliday48-62)

## Revolutionary War

### Declaration of Independence

[![Image 5](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence.jpg/250px-United_States_Declaration_of_Independence.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:United_States_Declaration_of_Independence.jpg)

The [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence"), which Jefferson largely wrote in isolation between June 11 and 28, 1776, from a floor he was renting in a home at 700 [Market Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_Street_(Philadelphia) "Market Street (Philadelphia)") in [Center City Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_City,_Philadelphia "Center City, Philadelphia"),[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-nps.gov-63) contain "the most potent and consequential words in American history," historian [Joseph Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ellis "Joseph Ellis") later wrote.

Jefferson was the primary author of the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence").[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-64) At age 33, he was one of the youngest delegates to the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress "Second Continental Congress"), which convened in the colonial capital of [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") following the [Battles of Lexington and Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord "Battles of Lexington and Concord"), which launched the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") in 1775. Delegates to the Congress overwhelmingly favored authoring, ratifying, and issuing a formal declaration of independence from Britain.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-65) Jefferson was inspired by the [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") ideals of the sanctity of the individual, and the writings of [Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke") and [Montesquieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montesquieu "Montesquieu").[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-66)

Jefferson sought out [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"), a Continental Congress delegate from [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Massachusetts_Bay "Province of Massachusetts Bay") and an emerging leader in the Congress.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-67) They became close friends, and Adams supported Jefferson's appointment to the [Committee of Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Five "Committee of Five"), which the Congress charged with authoring the Declaration: Adams, Jefferson, [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin "Benjamin Franklin"), [Robert R. Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_R._Livingston "Robert R. Livingston"), and [Roger Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Sherman "Roger Sherman"). The committee initially thought that Adams should write the document, but Adams persuaded the committee to choose Jefferson due to Jefferson being a Virginian, popular, and a good writer by Adams.[[e]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-69)

Jefferson consulted with his fellow committee members, but mostly wrote the Declaration of Independence in isolation between June 11 and 28, 1776.[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-nps.gov-63) Jefferson drew considerably on his proposed draft of the [Virginia Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Virginia "Constitution of Virginia"), [George Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason "George Mason")'s draft of the [Virginia Declaration of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Declaration_of_Rights "Virginia Declaration of Rights"), and other sources.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-70) Other committee members made some changes, and a final draft was presented to Congress on June 28, 1776.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham,_2012,_p._105-71) Congress began debate over its contents on Monday, July 1,[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham,_2012,_p._105-71) resulting in the removal of roughly a fourth of Jefferson's original draft.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-72)[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ellis,_1996,_p._50-73) Jefferson resented the changes, but he did not speak publicly about them.[[f]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-75) On July 4, 1776, the Congress voted unanimously to ratify the Declaration, and delegates signed it on August 2. Jefferson and the other delegates knew they were committing [high treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom "High treason in the United Kingdom") against [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown "The Crown"), which was punishable by [torture and death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered "Hanged, drawn and quartered").[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-76)

Following its ratification, the Declaration was released publicly. Two days after its ratification, on July 6, _[The Pennsylvania Evening Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Pennsylvania\_Evening\_Post "The Pennsylvania Evening Post")_, was the first newspaper to publish it. On July 8 at noon, it was read publicly and simultaneously for the first time at three designated locations: [Trenton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenton,_New_Jersey "Trenton, New Jersey"); [Easton, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easton,_Pennsylvania "Easton, Pennsylvania"); and Philadelphia.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-77)

Contemporary historians generally view the Declaration of Independence as one of the most significant and influential written documents in world history, and Jefferson's preamble is regarded as an enduring statement on [individual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_and_group_rights "Individual and group rights") and [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights "Human rights"). Jefferson's phrase "[all men are created equal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_men_are_created_equal "All men are created equal")" has been called "one of the best-known sentences in the English language". [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") history chairman [David Armitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Armitage_(historian) "David Armitage (historian)") has written that, "No American document has had a greater global impact than the Declaration of Independence",[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-78) and historian [Joseph Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ellis "Joseph Ellis") has written that the Declaration includes "the most potent and consequential words in American history".[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ellis,_1996,_p._50-73)[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-79)

### Virginia state legislator and governor

[![Image 6: Governor's Palace](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/The_Governor%27s_Palace_--_Williamsburg_%28VA%29_September_2012.jpg/250px-The_Governor%27s_Palace_--_Williamsburg_%28VA%29_September_2012.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Governor%27s_Palace_--_Williamsburg_(VA)_September_2012.jpg)

[Governor's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor%27s_Palace_(Williamsburg,_Virginia) "Governor's Palace (Williamsburg, Virginia)"), Jefferson's residence in [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Virginia "Williamsburg, Virginia") during his term as Virginia's governor from 1779 to 1781

At the start of the American Revolution, [Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_(United_States) "Colonel (United States)") Jefferson was named commander of the [Albemarle County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albemarle_County,_Virginia "Albemarle County, Virginia") Militia on September 26, 1775.[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-80) He was then elected to the [Virginia House of Delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_House_of_Delegates "Virginia House of Delegates") for Albemarle County in September 1776, when finalizing the state constitution was a priority.[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_pp._101-81)[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Fer26-82) For nearly three years, Jefferson assisted with the constitution and was especially proud of his Bill for Establishing Religious Freedom, which prohibited state support of religious institutions or enforcement of religious doctrine.[[77]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-83) The bill failed to pass, as did his legislation to disestablish the [Anglican Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Church "Anglican Church"), but both were later revived by [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison "James Madison").[[78]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-84)

In 1778, Jefferson was given the task of revising the state's laws. He drafted 126 bills in three years, including laws to streamline the judicial system. He proposed statutes that provided for general education, which he considered the basis of "republican government".[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_pp._101-81) Jefferson also was concerned that Virginia's powerful landed gentry were becoming a hereditary aristocracy and took the lead in abolishing what he called "feudal and unnatural distinctions".[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Brewer-85) He targeted laws such as [entail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entail "Entail") and [primogeniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primogeniture "Primogeniture") by which a deceased landowner's oldest son was vested with all land ownership and power.[[79]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Brewer-85)[[g]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-87)

Jefferson was elected [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Virginia "Governor of Virginia") for one-year terms in 1779 and 1780.[[81]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-88) He transferred the state capital from Williamsburg to [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia "Richmond, Virginia"), and introduced additional measures for public education, religious freedom, and inheritance.[[82]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-89)

During General [Benedict Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Arnold "Benedict Arnold")'s 1781 [invasion of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_on_Richmond "Raid on Richmond"), Jefferson escaped Richmond just ahead of the British forces, which razed the city.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-90)[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-91) He sent emergency dispatches to Colonel [Sampson Mathews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampson_Mathews "Sampson Mathews") and other commanders in an attempt to repel Arnold's efforts.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-92)[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-93) When the British occupied [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk,_Virginia "Norfolk, Virginia"), Jefferson had caused patriots to burn and destroy it in 1776.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-94)[General Charles Cornwallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Cornwallis,_1st_Marquess_Cornwallis "Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis") that spring dispatched a cavalry force led by [Banastre Tarleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banastre_Tarleton "Banastre Tarleton") to capture Jefferson and members of the Assembly at Monticello, but [Jack Jouett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Jouett "Jack Jouett") of the [Virginia militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_militia "Virginia militia") thwarted the British plan.[[h]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-97) Jefferson escaped to [Poplar Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poplar_Forest "Poplar Forest"), his plantation to the west.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-98) When the General Assembly reconvened in June 1781, it conducted an inquiry into Jefferson's actions which eventually concluded that Jefferson had acted with honor, but Jefferson was not reelected.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-99)

In April of the same year, his daughter Lucy died at age one. A second daughter of that name was born the following year, but she died at age two.[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-100)

In 1782, Jefferson refused a partnership offer by North Carolina Governor [Abner Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abner_Nash "Abner Nash"), in a profiteering scheme involving the sale of confiscated Loyalist lands.[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-101) Unlike some Founders, Jefferson was content with his Monticello estate and the land he owned in the vicinity of Virginia's [Shenandoah Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenandoah_Valley "Shenandoah Valley"). Jefferson thought of Monticello as an intellectual gathering place for [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison "James Madison"), [James Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Monroe "James Monroe"), and other friends.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-102)

Jefferson accused British-based merchants of unfairly depressing tobacco prices and forcing Virginia planters to take on unsustainable debt loads. In 1786, he remarked:

> A powerful engine for this [mercantile profiteering] was the giving of good prices and credit to the planter till they got him more immersed in debt than he could pay without selling lands or slaves. They then reduced the prices given for his tobacco so that…they never permitted him to clear off his debt.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-103)

### _Notes on the State of Virginia_

In 1780, Jefferson received a letter of inquiry from French diplomat [François Barbé-Marbois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Barb%C3%A9-Marbois "François Barbé-Marbois") into the geography, history, and government of Virginia, as part of a study of the United States. Jefferson organized his responses in a book, _Notes on the State of Virginia_ (1785).[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-104) The book explores what constitutes a good society, using Virginia as an exemplar. Jefferson included extensive data about the state's natural resources and economy and wrote at length about slavery and [miscegenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscegenation "Miscegenation"); he articulated his belief that blacks and whites could not live together as free people in one society because of justified resentments of the enslaved.[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-105) He also wrote of his views on the American Indians, equating them to European settlers.[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-106)[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-107)

_Notes_ was first published in 1785 in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_(language) "French (language)") and appeared in English in 1787.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-108) Biographer [George Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Tucker_(author) "George Tucker (author)") considered the work "surprising in the extent of the information which a single individual had been thus far able to acquire, as to the physical features of the state";[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-109)[University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia") historian [Merrill D. Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrill_D._Peterson "Merrill D. Peterson") described it as an accomplishment for which all Americans should be grateful.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-110)

## Member of Congress

[![Image 7: Legislative chamber](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Independence_Hall_Assembly_Room.jpg/250px-Independence_Hall_Assembly_Room.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Independence_Hall_Assembly_Room.jpg)

The Assembly Room at [Independence Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Hall "Independence Hall") in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"), where Jefferson served as a delegate to the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress "Second Continental Congress") and where the Congress edited but unanimously ratified his draft of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776

Jefferson was appointed a Virginia delegate to the [Congress of the Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Confederation "Congress of the Confederation") organized following the [peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783) "Treaty of Paris (1783)") with Great Britain in 1783. He was a member of the committee setting foreign exchange rates and [recommended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_for_Establishing_Uniformity_in_the_Coinage,_Weights,_and_Measures_of_the_United_States "Plan for Establishing Uniformity in the Coinage, Weights, and Measures of the United States") an American currency based on the decimal system that was adopted.[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-111) He advised the formation of the [Committee of the States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_the_States "Committee of the States") to fill the power vacuum when Congress was in recess.[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-112) The committee met when Congress adjourned, but disagreements rendered it dysfunctional.[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-113)

Jefferson sent a letter (revealed in 2025) to Virginia Governor [Benjamin Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Harrison_V "Benjamin Harrison V"), dated December 31, 1783, in which he relates the wave of enthusiasm of Europeans for taking up arms against their leaders. Jefferson was sharing the affirmation of his own promotion of the right to bear arms that brought about the American Revolution. The letter also conveys Jefferson's anxiety over the final ratification of the Treaty of Paris formally ending the revolutionary war. Signed initially by the parties in September, the still-outstanding consent of two colonies was required in London (then a two-month journey) by the following March. The deadline was ultimately but barely met, with the required signatures made in mid-January.[[106]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-114)

In the Congress's 1783–1784 session, Jefferson acted as chairman of committees to establish a viable system of government for the new Republic and to propose a policy for settlement of the western territories. He was the principal author of the [Land Ordinance of 1784](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Ordinance_of_1784 "Land Ordinance of 1784"), whereby Virginia ceded to the national government the vast area that it claimed northwest of the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River "Ohio River"). He insisted that this territory should not be used as colonial territory by any of the thirteen states, but that it should be divided into sections that could become states. He plotted borders for nine new states in their initial stages and wrote an ordinance banning slavery in all the nation's territories. Congress made extensive revisions and rejected the ban on slavery.[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peretson189-115)[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-116) The provisions banning slavery, known as the "Jefferson Proviso", were modified and implemented three years later in the [Northwest Ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Ordinance "Northwest Ordinance") of 1787 and became the law for the entire [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory "Northwest Territory").[[107]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peretson189-115)

## Minister to France

[![Image 8](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Grille_De_Chaillot.jpg/250px-Grille_De_Chaillot.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Grille_De_Chaillot.jpg)

A 1779 engraving of [Champs-Élysées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champs-%C3%89lys%C3%A9es "Champs-Élysées") seen through the Grille de Chaillot with Jefferson's residence in Paris on the left

On May 7, 1784, Jefferson was appointed by the [Congress of the Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Confederation "Congress of the Confederation")[[i]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-117) to join [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin "Benjamin Franklin") and [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") in Paris as Minister [Plenipotentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenipotentiary "Plenipotentiary") for Negotiating Treaties of Amity and Commerce with [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain "Kingdom of Great Britain") and other countries.[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-118)[[j]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-120) With his young daughter Patsy and two servants, he departed in July 1784, arriving in Paris the next month.[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Stewart39-121)[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-122) Jefferson had Patsy educated at [Pentemont Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentemont_Abbey "Pentemont Abbey"). Less than a year later, he was assigned the additional duty of succeeding Franklin as Minister to France. French foreign minister [Count de Vergennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Gravier,_comte_de_Vergennes "Charles Gravier, comte de Vergennes") commented, "You replace Monsieur Franklin, I hear." Jefferson replied, "I succeed. No man can replace him."[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-123) During his five years in Paris, Jefferson played a leading role in shaping [U.S. foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_United_States "Foreign policy of the United States").[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-124)

In 1786, he met and fell in love with [Maria Cosway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Cosway "Maria Cosway"), a married 27-year-old Italian-English musician. She returned to Great Britain after six weeks, but she and Jefferson maintained a lifelong correspondence.[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-125) During the summer of 1786, Jefferson arrived in London to meet with John Adams, who was then serving as the nation's first [US Ambassador to Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_the_United_Kingdom "List of ambassadors of the United States to the United Kingdom"). Adams had official access to [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III "George III") and arranged a meeting between Jefferson and the king. Jefferson later described the king's reception of the men as "ungracious". According to Adams's grandson, George III turned his back on both in a gesture of public insult. Jefferson returned to France in August.[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-126)

[![Image 9](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Mather_Brown_-_Thomas_Jefferson_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/250px-Mather_Brown_-_Thomas_Jefferson_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mather_Brown_-_Thomas_Jefferson_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

1786 portrait by [Mather Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mather_Brown "Mather Brown")

Jefferson sent for his youngest surviving child, nine-year-old Polly, in June 1787. She was accompanied by a young slave from Monticello, [Sally Hemings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Hemings "Sally Hemings"). Jefferson had taken her older brother, [James Hemings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hemings "James Hemings"), to Paris as part of his domestic staff and had him trained in French cuisine.[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-127) According to Sally's son, [Madison Hemings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Hemings "Madison Hemings"), 16-year-old Sally and Jefferson began a sexual relationship in Paris, where she became pregnant.[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-madisonstatement-128)[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham_pp._216-129) Madison indicated his mother agreed to return to the United States only after Jefferson promised to free her children when they came of age.[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-madisonstatement-128)[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham_pp._216-129)

While in France, Jefferson became a regular companion of the [Marquis de Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette "Marquis de Lafayette"), a [French hero of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_in_the_American_Revolutionary_War "France in the American Revolutionary War"), and Jefferson used his influence with Lafayette to procure trade agreements with France.[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Bowers328-130)[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Burstein120-131) As the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution") began, Jefferson agreed to allow his Paris residence at [Hôtel de Langeac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Langeac "Hôtel de Langeac") to be used for meetings by Lafayette and other revolutionary leaders. He was in Paris during the [storming of the Bastille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storming_of_the_Bastille "Storming of the Bastille") on July 14, 1789, and he consulted with Lafayette as Lafayette drafted the [Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_the_Rights_of_Man_and_of_the_Citizen "Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen").[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-132) Jefferson often found his mail opened by postmasters, so he invented his own enciphering device, the "[Wheel Cipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_disk "Jefferson disk")"; he wrote important communications in code for the rest of his career.[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-133)[[k]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-134) Unable to attend the 1787 Constitution Convention, Jefferson supported the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution "United States Constitution") but desired the addition of the promised Bill of Rights.[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-135) Jefferson left Paris for America in September 1789.[[125]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-136) He remained a firm supporter of the French Revolution while opposing its more violent elements.[[126]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-137)

## Secretary of State

[![Image 10: Painting of Jefferson](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Thomas_Jefferson_by_Peale_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Thomas_Jefferson_by_Peale_%28cropped%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Thomas_Jefferson_by_Peale_(cropped).jpg)

A 48-year-old Jefferson in 1791, in a portrait by [Charles Willson Peale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Willson_Peale "Charles Willson Peale")

Soon after returning from France, Jefferson accepted President Washington's invitation to serve as [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State "United States Secretary of State").[[127]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-138) Pressing issues at the time, the national debt and where the new national capital should be placed following its planned relocation from [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") in 1800, placed him at odds with [Secretary of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Treasury "United States Secretary of the Treasury")[Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton "Alexander Hamilton"), who favored a capital close to major commercial centers in the [Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_United_States "Northeastern United States"), while Washington, Jefferson, and other agrarians wanted it further south.[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cooke,_1970,_pp._523%E2%80%9345-139) After lengthy deadlock, the [Compromise of 1790](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1790 "Compromise of 1790") was struck, permanently locating the capital on the [Potomac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River "Potomac River"), and the federal government assumed the war debts of all original [13 states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies "Thirteen Colonies").[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cooke,_1970,_pp._523%E2%80%9345-139)

Jefferson opposed a national debt, preferring that each state retire its own, which contrasted with Hamilton's vision of the [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States") consolidating state debts[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-140) and establishing national credit and a national bank. Jefferson strenuously opposed both policies and attempted to undermine Hamilton's agenda, which nearly led Washington to dismiss him from the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States "Cabinet of the United States"). He later left the cabinet voluntarily.[[130]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-141)

Jefferson's goals were to decrease American dependence on British commerce and to expand commercial trade with France. He sought to weaken [Spanish colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas "Spanish colonization of the Americas") of the [Trans-Appalachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Appalachia "Trans-Appalachia") and British control in the North, believing this would aid in the pacification of [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States "Native Americans in the United States").[[131]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-142)

Along with political protegé [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison "James Madison"), then a [U.S. Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives"), and author [Philip Freneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Freneau "Philip Freneau"), Jefferson co-founded the _[National Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National\_Gazette "National Gazette")_ in Philadelphia in 1791, which sought to counter the policies of the [Federalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist_Party "Federalist Party"), which Hamilton was promoting through the _[Gazette of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazette\_of\_the\_United\_States "Gazette of the United States")_, an influential Federalist newspaper. The _National Gazette_ criticized the policies promoted by Hamilton, often in anonymous essays signed by the pen name _Brutus_ at Jefferson's urging and written by Madison.[[132]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-143) In the spring of 1791, Jefferson was suffering from [migraines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migraine "Migraine") and tiring of the infighting with Hamilton, so he and Madison departed for a vacation in [Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermont "Vermont").[[133]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Randall_1996_p1-144)

In May 1792, Jefferson's concern about emerging political rivalries in the young nation was escalating, and he wrote Washington, imploring him to run for reelection for a second term [that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1792_United_States_presidential_election "1792 United States presidential election") as a unifying influence.[[134]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-145) He urged the president to rally the citizenry to a party that would defend democracy against the corrupting influence of banks and financial-focused interests, which the Federalists were embracing and espousing. Historians recognize Jefferson's letter to Washington as one of the first delineations of [Democratic-Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-Republican_Party "Democratic-Republican Party") principles.[[135]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-146) Jefferson, Madison, and other Democratic-Republican organizers favored [states' rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States%27_rights "States' rights") and local control and opposed the federal concentration of power. Hamilton, conversely, sought more power vested in the federal government.[[136]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-147)

Jefferson supported France against Britain when the two nations fought in 1793, though his arguments in Washington's Cabinet were undercut by French Revolutionary envoy [Edmond-Charles Genêt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond-Charles_Gen%C3%AAt "Edmond-Charles Genêt")'s open scorn for Washington.[[137]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-148) In discussions with British Minister [George Hammond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hammond_(diplomat) "George Hammond (diplomat)"), Jefferson tried in vain to persuade the British to vacate their posts in the Northwest and to compensate the U.S. for enslaved people freed by the British at the end of the Revolutionary War. Jefferson also sought to return to private life, and resigned from the cabinet in December 1793; he may also have wanted to bolster his political influence from outside the administration.[[138]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-149)

After the [Washington administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_Washington "Presidency of George Washington") negotiated the [Jay Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Treaty "Jay Treaty") with Britain in 1794, Jefferson saw a cause around which he could rally the Democratic-Republican Party. He organized national opposition to the treaty from Monticello.[[139]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-150) The treaty, designed by Hamilton, aimed to reduce tensions and increase trade. Jefferson warned that it would increase British influence and subvert republicanism, calling it "the boldest act [Hamilton and Jay] ever ventured on to undermine the government".[[140]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-151) The Treaty passed, but it expired in 1805 during Jefferson's presidential administration, and then President Jefferson did not renew it. Jefferson continued his pro-France stance; during the violence of the [Reign of Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reign_of_Terror "Reign of Terror"), he declined to disavow the revolution. "To back away from France would be to undermine the cause of republicanism in America", he wrote.[[141]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-152)

## Election of 1796 and vice presidency

[![Image 11: Electoral College map](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/ElectoralCollege1796.svg/250px-ElectoralCollege1796.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ElectoralCollege1796.svg)

The results of the [1796 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1796_United_States_presidential_election "1796 United States presidential election") between Adams and Jefferson, won by Adams

In the [presidential campaign of 1796](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1796_United_States_presidential_election "1796 United States presidential election"), Jefferson lost the electoral college vote to Federalist [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") 71–68. He did, however, receive the second-highest number of votes and, under the electoral laws at the time, was elected as vice president. As presiding officer of the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate"), Jefferson assumed a more passive role than his predecessor, John Adams. He allowed the Senate to freely conduct debates and confined his participation to procedural issues, which he called an "honorable and easy" role.[[142]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-153) Jefferson previously studied parliamentary law and procedure for 40 years, making him qualified to serve as presiding officer. In 1800, he published his assembled notes on Senate procedure as _[A Manual of Parliamentary Practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson%27s\_Manual "Jefferson's Manual")_.[[143]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-154) He cast only three [tie-breaking votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tie-breaking_votes_cast_by_the_vice_president_of_the_United_States "List of tie-breaking votes cast by the vice president of the United States") in the Senate.

In four confidential talks with French consul Joseph Létombe in the spring of 1797, Jefferson attacked Adams, predicting that his rival would only serve one term. He also encouraged France to invade England, and advised Létombe to stall any American envoys sent to Paris.[[144]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-155) This toughened the tone that the French government adopted toward the [Adams administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_John_Adams "Presidency of John Adams"). After Adams's initial peace envoys were rebuffed, Jefferson and his supporters lobbied for the release of papers related to the incident, called the [XYZ Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XYZ_Affair "XYZ Affair") after the letters used to disguise the identities of the French officials involved.[[145]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-156) But the tactic backfired when it was revealed that French officials had demanded bribes, rallying public support against France. The U.S. began an undeclared naval war with France known as the [Quasi-War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-War "Quasi-War").[[146]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-157)

During the Adams presidency, the Federalists rebuilt the military, levied new taxes, and enacted the [Alien and Sedition Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_and_Sedition_Acts "Alien and Sedition Acts"). Jefferson believed these laws were intended to suppress Democratic-Republicans, rather than prosecute enemy aliens, and considered them unconstitutional.[[147]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-158) To rally opposition, he and James Madison anonymously wrote the [Kentucky and Virginia Resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_and_Virginia_Resolutions "Kentucky and Virginia Resolutions"), asserting that the federal government had no right to exercise powers not specifically delegated to it by the states.[[148]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-159) The resolutions followed the "[interposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interposition "Interposition")" approach of Madison, that states may shield their citizens from federal laws that they deem unconstitutional. Jefferson advocated [nullification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullification_(U.S._Constitution) "Nullification (U.S. Constitution)"), allowing states to entirely invalidate federal laws.[[149]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-160)[[l]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-162) He warned that, "unless arrested at the threshold", the Alien and Sedition Acts would "drive these states into revolution and blood".[[151]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-163)

Biographer [Ron Chernow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Chernow "Ron Chernow") contends that "the theoretical damage of the Kentucky and Virginia Resolutions was deep and lasting, and was a recipe for disunion", and contributed to the outbreak of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") and later events.[[152]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-164) Washington was so appalled by the resolutions that he told [Patrick Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Henry "Patrick Henry") that, if "systematically and pertinaciously pursued", the resolutions would "dissolve the union or produce coercion".[[153]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Chernow587-165) Jefferson had always admired Washington's leadership skills but felt that his Federalist party was leading the country in the wrong direction. He decided not to attend Washington's funeral in 1799 because of acute differences with him while serving as secretary of state.[[154]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-166)

### Election of 1800

[![Image 12: Electoral College map](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/ElectoralCollege1800.svg/250px-ElectoralCollege1800.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ElectoralCollege1800.svg)

The results of the [1800 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_United_States_presidential_election "1800 United States presidential election") between [Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") and Jefferson, which Jefferson won, making him the nation's third [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States")

Jefferson ran for president against [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") again in [1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_United_States_presidential_election "1800 United States presidential election"). Adams' campaign was weakened by unpopular taxes and vicious Federalist infighting over his actions in the Quasi-War.[[155]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-167) Democratic-Republicans pointed to the Alien and Sedition Acts and accused the Federalists of being secret pro-Britain monarchists. Federalists, in turn, charged that Jefferson was a godless libertine beholden to the French.[[156]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-168)[UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles") history professor [Joyce Appleby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Appleby "Joyce Appleby") described the 1800 presidential election as "one of the most acrimonious in the annals of American history".[[157]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-169)

The Democratic-Republicans ultimately won more electoral college votes, due in part to the electors that resulted from the addition of three-fifths of the South's slaves to the population calculation under the [Three-Fifths Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-Fifths_Compromise "Three-Fifths Compromise").[[158]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-170) Jefferson and his vice presidential candidate [Aaron Burr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Burr "Aaron Burr") unexpectedly received an equal total. Because of the tie, the election was decided by the Federalist-dominated [U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives").[[159]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-171)[[m]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-173) Hamilton lobbied Federalist representatives on Jefferson's behalf, believing him a lesser political evil than Burr. On February 17, 1801, after thirty-six ballots, the House elected Jefferson president and Burr vice president.[[160]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-W284-172)

The win led to Democratic-Republican celebrations throughout the country.[[161]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-174) Some of Jefferson's opponents argued that he owed his victory to the South's inflated number of electors.[[162]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-175) Others alleged that Jefferson secured [James Asheton Bayard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Bayard_(elder) "James A. Bayard (elder)")'s tie-breaking electoral vote by promising to retain various Federalist posts in the government.[[160]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-W284-172) Jefferson disputed the allegation, and the historical record is inconclusive.[[163]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-176)

The transition proceeded smoothly, marking a watershed in American history. Historian [Gordon S. Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_S._Wood "Gordon S. Wood") writes that, "it was one of the first popular elections in modern history that resulted in the [peaceful transfer of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power "Peaceful transition of power") from one 'party' to another."[[160]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-W284-172)

## Presidency (1801–1809)

[![Image 13](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Reproduction-of-the-1805-Rembrandt-Peale-painting-of-Thomas-Jefferson-New-York-Historical-Society_1.jpg/250px-Reproduction-of-the-1805-Rembrandt-Peale-painting-of-Thomas-Jefferson-New-York-Historical-Society_1.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Reproduction-of-the-1805-Rembrandt-Peale-painting-of-Thomas-Jefferson-New-York-Historical-Society_1.jpg)

_Thomas Jefferson_, an 1805 portrait by [Rembrandt Peale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rembrandt_Peale "Rembrandt Peale")

Jefferson was [sworn in as president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Thomas_Jefferson "First inauguration of Thomas Jefferson") by [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_United_States "Chief Justice of the United States")[John Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Marshall "John Marshall") at the new [Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Capitol "United States Capitol") in Washington, D.C., on March 4, 1801. His inauguration was not attended by outgoing President Adams. In contrast to his two predecessors, Jefferson exhibited a dislike of formal etiquette. Plainly dressed, he chose to walk alongside friends to the Capitol from his nearby boardinghouse instead of arriving by carriage.[[164]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-177) His inaugural address struck a note of reconciliation and commitment to democratic ideology, declaring, "We have been called by different names brethren of the same principle. We are all Republicans, we are all Federalists."[[165]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-178)[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson41-179) Ideologically, he stressed "equal and exact justice to all men", minority rights, and freedom of speech, religion, and press.[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_2002_p40-180) He said that a free and republican government was "the strongest government on earth".[[167]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_2002_p40-180) He nominated moderate Republicans to his cabinet: James Madison as secretary of state, [Henry Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dearborn "Henry Dearborn") as secretary of war, [Levi Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Lincoln_Sr. "Levi Lincoln Sr.") as attorney general, and [Robert Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Smith_(Cabinet_member) "Robert Smith (Cabinet member)") as secretary of the navy.[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson41-179)

Widowed since 1782, Jefferson first relied on his two daughters to serve as his official hostesses.[[168]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendricks201521%E2%80%9322-181) In late May 1801, he asked [Dolley Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolley_Madison "Dolley Madison"), wife of his long-time friend James Madison, to be the permanent White House hostess. She was also in charge of the completion of the White House mansion. Dolley served as White House hostess for the rest of Jefferson's two terms and then for another eight years as First Lady while her husband was president.[[168]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendricks201521%E2%80%9322-181)

### Financial affairs

[![Image 14](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Albert_Gallatin_%28by_Gilbert_Stuart%29.jpg/250px-Albert_Gallatin_%28by_Gilbert_Stuart%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Albert_Gallatin_(by_Gilbert_Stuart).jpg)

[Albert Gallatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Gallatin "Albert Gallatin"), Jefferson's [Treasury Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Treasury "United States Secretary of the Treasury"), depicted in a portrait by [Gilbert Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Stuart "Gilbert Stuart")

Jefferson's first challenge as president was shrinking the $83 million national debt.[[169]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-182) He began dismantling Hamilton's Federalist fiscal system with help from the secretary of the treasury, Albert Gallatin.[[166]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson41-179) Gallatin devised a plan to eliminate the national debt in sixteen years by extensive annual appropriations and reduction in taxes.[[170]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_pp._43-44-183) The administration eliminated the whiskey excise and other taxes after closing "unnecessary offices" and cutting "useless establishments and expenses".[[171]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Wood,_2010,_p._293-184)[[172]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-185)

Jefferson believed that the [First Bank of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bank_of_the_United_States "First Bank of the United States") represented a "most deadly hostility" to republican government.[[170]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_pp._43-44-183) He wanted to dismantle the bank before its charter expired in 1811, but was dissuaded by Gallatin.[[173]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-186) Gallatin argued that the national bank was a useful financial institution and set out to expand its operations.[[174]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_p._44-187) Jefferson looked to other corners to address the growing national debt.[[174]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_p._44-187) He shrank the Navy, for example, deeming it unnecessary in peacetime, and incorporated a fleet of inexpensive gunboats intended only for local defense to avoid provocation against foreign powers.[[171]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Wood,_2010,_p._293-184) After two terms, he had lowered the national debt from $83 million to $57 million.[[175]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham387-188)

### Domestic affairs

Jefferson pardoned several of those imprisoned under the Alien and Sedition Acts.[[176]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-189) Congressional Republicans repealed the [Judiciary Act of 1801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Judges_Act "Midnight Judges Act"), which removed nearly all of Adams's "midnight judges". A subsequent appointment battle led to the Supreme Court's landmark decision in _[Marbury v. Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marbury\_v.\_Madison "Marbury v. Madison")_, asserting judicial review over executive branch actions.[[177]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham,_2012,_p._413-190) Jefferson appointed three [Supreme Court justices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Associate Justice of the Supreme Court of the United States"): [William Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Johnson_(judge) "William Johnson (judge)") (1804), [Henry Brockholst Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Brockholst_Livingston "Henry Brockholst Livingston") (1807), and [Thomas Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Todd "Thomas Todd") (1807).[[178]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-191)

Jefferson strongly felt the need for a national military university, producing an officer engineering corps for a national defense based on the advancement of the sciences, rather than having to rely on foreign sources.[[179]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-192) He signed the [Military Peace Establishment Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Peace_Establishment_Act "Military Peace Establishment Act") on March 16, 1802, founding the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy "United States Military Academy") at [West Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point,_New_York "West Point, New York"). The act documented a new set of laws and limits for the military. Jefferson was also hoping to bring reform to the Executive branch, replacing Federalists and active opponents throughout the officer corps to promote Republican values.[[180]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-193)

Jefferson took great interest in the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress"), which had been established in 1800. He often recommended books to acquire. In 1802, Congress authorized Jefferson to name the first Librarian of Congress, and formed a committee to establish library regulations. Congress also granted both the president and vice president the right to use the library.[[181]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-194)

### Foreign affairs (1801–1805)

#### First Barbary War

[![Image 15: Map. Barbary Coast of North Africa 1806](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/1800_map_Afrique_by_Arrowsmith_BPL_15210_detail2.jpg/250px-1800_map_Afrique_by_Arrowsmith_BPL_15210_detail2.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1800_map_Afrique_by_Arrowsmith_BPL_15210_detail2.jpg)

The [Barbary Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Coast "Barbary Coast") of [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa") in 1806, including (left to right): [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco"), [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar "Gibraltar"), [Tunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunis "Tunis"), and [Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli,_Libya "Tripoli, Libya")

American merchant ships had been protected from [Barbary Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Coast "Barbary Coast") pirates by the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") when the states were British colonies.[[182]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-195) After independence, however, pirates often captured U.S. merchant ships, pillaged cargoes, and enslaved or held crew members for ransom. Jefferson had opposed paying tribute to the Barbary States since 1785. In 1801, he authorized a U.S. Navy fleet under Commodore [Richard Dale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dale "Richard Dale") to make a show of force in the Mediterranean, the first American naval squadron to cross the Atlantic.[[183]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham_pp._364-196) Following the fleet's first engagement, he successfully asked Congress for a declaration of war.[[183]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham_pp._364-196) The "First Barbary War" was the first foreign war fought by the U.S.[[184]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-197)

[Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha "Pasha") of [Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli,_Libya "Tripoli, Libya")[Yusuf Karamanli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Karamanli "Yusuf Karamanli") captured the [USS _Philadelphia_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Philadelphia_(1799) "USS Philadelphia (1799)"), so Jefferson authorized [William Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Eaton_(soldier) "William Eaton (soldier)"), the U.S. Consul to [Tunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunis "Tunis"), to lead a force to restore the pasha's older brother to the throne.[[185]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-198) The American navy forced Tunis and Algiers into breaking their alliance with Tripoli. Jefferson ordered five separate naval bombardments of Tripoli, leading the pasha to sign a treaty that restored peace in the Mediterranean.[[186]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-199) This victory proved only temporary, but according to Wood, "many Americans celebrated it as a vindication of their policy of spreading free trade around the world and as a great victory for liberty over tyranny."[[187]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-200)

#### Louisiana Purchase

[![Image 16](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Louisiana_Purchase.png/250px-Louisiana_Purchase.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Louisiana_Purchase.png)

The 1803 [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase"), completed during [Jefferson's presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Thomas_Jefferson "Presidency of Thomas Jefferson"), added 827,987 [square miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile "Square mile") (2,144,480 square kilometres), which doubled the geographic size of the United States.

Spain [ceded ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Treaty_of_San_Ildefonso "Third Treaty of San Ildefonso") of the Louisiana territory in 1800 to France. Jefferson was concerned that [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon")'s interests in the vast territory would threaten the security of the continent and [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River "Mississippi River") shipping. He wrote that the cession "works most sorely on the U.S. It completely reverses all the political relations of the U.S."[[188]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-201) In 1802, he instructed [James Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Monroe "James Monroe") and [Robert R. Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_R._Livingston_(chancellor) "Robert R. Livingston (chancellor)") to negotiate the purchase of [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans") and adjacent coastal areas.[[189]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-202) In early 1803, Jefferson offered Napoleon nearly $10 million for 40,000 square miles (100,000 square kilometres) of tropical territory.[[190]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Freehling2005-203)

Napoleon realized that French military control was impractical over such a vast remote territory, and he was in dire need of funds for his [wars on the home front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars"). In early April 1803, he unexpectedly made negotiators a counter-offer to sell 827,987 square miles (2,144,480 square kilometres) of French territory for $15 million (~$380 million in 2024), doubling the size of the United States.[[190]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Freehling2005-203) U.S. negotiators accepted the offer and signed the treaty on April 30, 1803.[[175]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham387-188) Word of the unexpected purchase did not reach Jefferson until July 3, 1803.[[175]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham387-188) He unknowingly acquired the most fertile tract of land of its size on Earth, making the new country self-sufficient in food and other resources. The sale also significantly curtailed European presence in North America, removing obstacles to U.S. [westward expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_the_United_States "Territorial evolution of the United States").[[191]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ellis208-204)

Most thought that this was an exceptional opportunity, despite Republican reservations about the Constitutional authority of the federal government to acquire land.[[192]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-The_Rise_of_American_Democracy-205) Jefferson initially thought that a Constitutional [amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Constitution#Senate_changes "History of the United States Constitution") was necessary to purchase and govern the new territory; but he later changed his mind, fearing that this would give cause to oppose the purchase, and urged a speedy debate and ratification.[[193]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-206) On October 20, 1803, the Senate ratified the purchase treaty by a vote of 24–7.[[194]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-207) Jefferson personally was humble about acquiring the Louisiana Territory, but he resented complainers who called the vast domain a "howling wilderness".[[195]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-208)

After the purchase, Jefferson preserved the region's Spanish legal code and instituted a gradual approach to integrating settlers into American democracy. He believed that a period of the federal rule would be necessary while Louisianans adjusted to their new nation.[[196]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-209)[[n]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-211) Historians have differed in their assessments regarding the constitutional implications of the sale,[[198]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-212) but they typically hail the Louisiana acquisition as a major accomplishment. [Frederick Jackson Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Jackson_Turner "Frederick Jackson Turner") called the purchase the most formative event in American history.[[191]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ellis208-204)

### Expeditions

[![Image 17: Corps of Discover on river boat October 1805](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Lewis_and_clark-expedition.jpg/250px-Lewis_and_clark-expedition.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lewis_and_clark-expedition.jpg)

_Lewis and Clark on the Lower Columbia_, a 1905 portrait by [Charles Marion Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Marion_Russell "Charles Marion Russell") depicting [Lewis and Clark's expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition "Lewis and Clark Expedition") on the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River "Columbia River") during Jefferson's presidency

Jefferson anticipated further westward settlements due to the Louisiana Purchase and arranged for the exploration and mapping of the uncharted territory. He sought to establish a U.S. claim ahead of competing European interests and to find the rumored [Northwest Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage "Northwest Passage").[[199]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ambrose76-213) Jefferson and others were influenced by exploration accounts of [Le Page du Pratz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine-Simon_Le_Page_du_Pratz "Antoine-Simon Le Page du Pratz") in Louisiana (1763) and [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook "James Cook") in the Pacific (1784),[[200]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-214) and they persuaded Congress in 1804 to fund an expedition to explore and [map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lewis_and_Clark_Expidition_Map.jpg "File:Lewis and Clark Expidition Map.jpg") the newly acquired territory to the Pacific Ocean.[[201]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-215)

Jefferson appointed secretary [Meriwether Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriwether_Lewis "Meriwether Lewis") and acquaintance [William Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clark "William Clark") to lead the [Corps of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_of_Discovery "Corps of Discovery") (1803–1806).[[202]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-216) In the months leading up to the expedition, Jefferson tutored Lewis in the sciences of mapping, botany, natural history, mineralogy, and astronomy and navigation, giving him unlimited access to his library at Monticello, which included the largest collection of books in the world on the subject of the geography and natural history of the North American continent, along with an impressive collection of maps.[[203]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-217) The expedition [lasted from May 1804 to September 1806](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition "Timeline of the Lewis and Clark Expedition") and obtained a wealth of scientific and geographic knowledge, including knowledge of many Indian tribes.[[204]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-218)

Jefferson organized three other western expeditions: the [William Dunbar and George Hunter Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar_and_Hunter_Expedition "Dunbar and Hunter Expedition") on the [Ouachita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_River "Ouachita River") (1804–1805), the [Thomas Freeman and Peter Custis Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Expedition_(1806) "Red River Expedition (1806)") (1806) on the [Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_South "Red River of the South"), and the [Zebulon Pike Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pike_Expedition "Pike Expedition") (1806–1807) into the Rocky Mountains and the Southwest. All three produced valuable information about the American frontier.[[205]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-219) This interest also motivated Jefferson to meet the Prussian explorer [Alexander von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt "Alexander von Humboldt") several times in June 1804, inquiring into Humboldt's knowledge of New Spain's natural resources, economic prospects, and demographic development.[[206]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-220)

### Native American affairs

[![Image 18](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Black_Hoof.jpg/250px-Black_Hoof.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Black_Hoof.jpg)

[Black Hoof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hoof "Black Hoof"), leader of the [Shawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee "Shawnee"), accepted Jefferson's Indian assimilation policies.

Jefferson refuted the contemporary notion that Indians were inferior and maintained that they were equal in body and mind to people of European descent,[[207]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-221) although he believed them to be inferior in terms of culture and technology.[[208]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-222) As governor of Virginia during the Revolutionary War, Jefferson recommended moving the [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee "Cherokee") and [Shawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee "Shawnee") tribes, who had allied with the British, to west of the Mississippi River. But when he took office as president, he quickly took measures to avert another major conflict, as American and Indian societies were in collision and the British were inciting Indian tribes from Canada.[[209]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Miller2008-223)[[210]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Sheehan1974-224) In Georgia, he stipulated that the state would release its legal claims for lands to its west in exchange for military support in expelling the Cherokee from Georgia. This facilitated his policy of western expansion, to "advance compactly as we multiply".[[211]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-225)

In keeping with his [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") thinking, President Jefferson adopted an assimilation policy toward American Indians known as his "civilization program" which included securing peaceful U.S.–Indian treaty alliances and encouraging agriculture. Jefferson advocated that Indian tribes should make federal purchases by credit holding their lands as collateral. Various tribes accepted Jefferson's policies, including the Shawnees led by [Black Hoof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hoof "Black Hoof"), the [Muscogee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscogee "Muscogee"), and the Cherokee. However, some Shawnees, led by [Tecumseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh "Tecumseh"), broke off from Black Hoof, and opposed Jefferson's assimilation policies.[[212]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-226)

Historian Bernard Sheehan argues that Jefferson believed that assimilation was best for American Indians, and next-best was removal to the west; he felt that the worst outcome of the conflict would be their attacking the whites.[[210]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Sheehan1974-224) Jefferson told [U.S. Secretary of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_War "United States Secretary of War")[Henry Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dearborn "Henry Dearborn"), who then oversaw Indian affairs, "If we are constrained to lift the hatchet against any tribe, we will never lay it down until that tribe is exterminated or driven beyond the Mississippi."[[213]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-227) Miller agrees that Jefferson believed that Indians should assimilate to American customs and agriculture. Historians such as [Peter S. Onuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_S._Onuf "Peter S. Onuf") and Merrill D. Peterson argue that Jefferson's actual Indian policies did little to promote assimilation and were a pretext to seize lands.[[214]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-228)

### Re-election in 1804 and second term

[![Image 19: Electoral College map](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/ElectoralCollege1804.svg/250px-ElectoralCollege1804.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ElectoralCollege1804.svg)

Results from the [1804 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1804_United_States_presidential_election "1804 United States presidential election") in which Jefferson was reelected overwhelmingly to a second term as president

Jefferson was nominated for reelection by the Democratic-Republican Party, with [George Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_(vice_president) "George Clinton (vice president)") replacing Burr as his running mate.[[215]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham405-229) The Federalist Party ran [Charles Cotesworth Pinckney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Cotesworth_Pinckney "Charles Cotesworth Pinckney") of South Carolina, John Adams's vice presidential candidate in the 1800 election. The Jefferson-Clinton ticket won overwhelmingly in the electoral college vote, by 162 to 14, promoting their achievement of a strong economy, lower taxes, and the Louisiana Purchase.[[215]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham405-229)

In March 1806, a split developed in the Democratic-Republican Party, led by fellow Virginian and former Republican ally [John Randolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Randolph_of_Roanoke "John Randolph of Roanoke"), who viciously accused President Jefferson on the floor of the House of moving too far in the Federalist direction, permanently setting Randolph apart [politically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertium_quids "Tertium quids") from Jefferson. Jefferson and Madison backed resolutions to limit or ban British imports in retaliation for British seizures of American shipping. Also, in 1808, Jefferson was the first president to propose a broad federal plan to build roads and canals across several states, asking for $20 million, further alarming Randolph and believers of limited government.[[216]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-230)

Jefferson's popularity suffered further in his second term as a result of his response to wars in Europe. Relations with Britain deteriorated, due partly to the antipathy between Jefferson and British diplomat [Anthony Merry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Merry "Anthony Merry"). After [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon")'s decisive victory at the [Battle of Austerlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Austerlitz "Battle of Austerlitz") in 1805, Napoleon became more aggressive in his negotiations with Jefferson and the U.S. over trading rights, which the U.S. proved unsuccessful in countering. Jefferson then led the enactment of the [Embargo Act of 1807](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo_Act_of_1807 "Embargo Act of 1807"), directed at both France and Britain, which triggered economic chaos in the U.S. and was strongly criticized, leading Jefferson to abandon the policy a year later.[[217]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-231)

During the American Revolution, colonial states abolished the international slave trade, but [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina "South Carolina") reopened it. In his annual message of December 1806, Jefferson denounced the international slave trade as "violations of human rights" and called on the new Congress to immediately criminalize it. The following year, in 1807, Congress passed the [Act Prohibiting Importation of Slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_Prohibiting_Importation_of_Slaves "Act Prohibiting Importation of Slaves"), which Jefferson signed.[[218]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-232)[[219]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Randal583-233) The act established severe punishment against the international slave trade, although it did not address the issue domestically.[[220]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-234)

In [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti "Haiti"), Jefferson's neutrality allowed arms to flow to the slave independence movement during the [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution "Haitian Revolution"), and Jefferson blocked attempts to assist Napoleon, who was defeated militarily in Haiti in 1803.[[221]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-jstorhaiti-235) But Jefferson's administration refused official recognition of Haiti during his second term, in deference to southern complaints about racial violence against slave holders. Recognition was not extended to Haiti until 1862.[[222]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-236)

### Controversies

#### Burr conspiracy and trial

[![Image 20](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/John_Vanderlyn_-_Official_Portrait_of_Vice_President_Aaron_Burr.jpg/250px-John_Vanderlyn_-_Official_Portrait_of_Vice_President_Aaron_Burr.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_Vanderlyn_-_Official_Portrait_of_Vice_President_Aaron_Burr.jpg)

An 1802 portrait of [Aaron Burr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Burr "Aaron Burr") by [John Vanderlyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vanderlyn "John Vanderlyn")

Following the 1801 electoral deadlock, Jefferson's relationship with his vice president, [Aaron Burr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Burr "Aaron Burr"), rapidly eroded. Jefferson suspected Burr of seeking the presidency for himself, while Burr was angered by Jefferson's refusal to appoint some of his supporters to federal office. Burr was dropped from the Democratic-Republican ticket in 1804 in favor of charismatic [George Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_(vice_president) "George Clinton (vice president)").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

The same year, Burr was soundly defeated in his bid to be elected [New York governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_York "Governor of New York"). During the campaign, [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton "Alexander Hamilton") made publicly callous remarks regarding Burr's moral character.[[223]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Chernow04_p714-237)[Burr challenged Hamilton to a duel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burr%E2%80%93Hamilton_duel "Burr–Hamilton duel"), held on July 11, 1804. In the duel, Burr mortally wounded Hamilton, who died the following day. Burr was subsequently indicted for Hamilton's murder, causing him to flee to [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(U.S._state) "Georgia (U.S. state)"), even though he remained president of the [U.S. Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") during Supreme Court Justice [Samuel Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Chase "Samuel Chase")'s [impeachment trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_of_Samuel_Chase "Impeachment of Samuel Chase").[[224]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-238) Both indictments quietly died and Burr was not prosecuted.

In August 1804, Burr contacted British Minister [Anthony Merry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Merry "Anthony Merry") offering to cede U.S. western territory in return for money and British ships.[[226]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-The_Burr_Conspiracy_2000-240) After leaving office in April 1805, Burr traveled west and conspired with Louisiana Territory governor [James Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wilkinson "James Wilkinson"), beginning a large-scale recruitment for a military expedition.[[227]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_2002_p50-241) Burr discussed seizing control of Mexico or Spanish Florida, or forming a secessionist state in New Orleans or the Western U.S.; historians remain unclear as to his true goal.[[228]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-242)[[o]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-244) In the fall of 1806, Burr launched a military flotilla carrying about 60 men down the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River "Ohio River"). Wilkinson renounced the plot and reported Burr's expedition to Jefferson, who ordered Burr's arrest.[[227]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_2002_p50-241)[[230]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-245) On February 13, 1807, Burr was captured in Louisiana and sent to Virginia to be tried for treason.[[226]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-The_Burr_Conspiracy_2000-240) Burr's 1807 conspiracy trial became a national issue.[[232]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-247) Jefferson attempted to preemptively influence the verdict by telling Congress that Burr's guilt was "beyond question", but the case came before his longtime political foe, and distant cousin, [John Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Marshall "John Marshall"), who dismissed the treason charge. Burr's legal team subpoenaed Jefferson, but Jefferson refused to testify, making the first argument for [executive privilege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_privilege "Executive privilege").[[233]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-248) Instead, Jefferson provided relevant legal documents.[[234]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-249) After a three-month trial, the jury found Burr not guilty, while Jefferson denounced his acquittal.[[235]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-250)[[p]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-251)[[236]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-252) Jefferson subsequently removed Wilkinson as territorial governor but retained him in the U.S. military. Historian James N. Banner criticized Jefferson for continuing to trust Wilkinson, a "faithless plotter".

#### Wilkinson's misconduct

Commanding General [James Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wilkinson "James Wilkinson") was a holdover of the Washington and Adams administrations. In 1804, Wilkinson received 12,000 pesos from the Spanish for information on American boundary plans. Wilkinson also received advances on his salary and payments on claims submitted to Secretary of War [Henry Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dearborn "Henry Dearborn"). This damaging information apparently was unknown to Jefferson. In 1805, Jefferson trusted Wilkinson and appointed him Louisiana Territory governor, admiring Wilkinson's work ethic.

In January 1806, Jefferson received information from Kentucky U.S. Attorney Joseph Davies that Wilkinson was on the Spanish payroll. Jefferson took no action against Wilkinson, since there was not then significant evidence against him. An investigation by the [U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") in December 1807 exonerated Wilkinson. In 1808, a military court looked into the allegations against Wilkinson but also found a lack of evidence. Jefferson retained Wilkinson in the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army"). Evidence found in Spanish archives in the 20th century proved Wilkinson was on the Spanish payroll.

### Foreign affairs (1805–1809)

#### Attempted annexation of Florida

In the aftermath of the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase"), Jefferson attempted to annex [West Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_West_Florida "Spanish West Florida") from Spain. In his annual message to Congress, on December 3, 1805, Jefferson railed against Spain over Florida border depredations.[[242]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson2002p49-258) A few days later Jefferson secretly requested a two-million-dollar expenditure to purchase Florida. Floor leader [John Randolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Randolph_of_Roanoke "John Randolph of Roanoke") opposed annexation, was upset over Jefferson's secrecy on the matter, and believed the money would end up going to Napoleon.[[242]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson2002p49-258) The Two Million Dollar bill passed only after Jefferson successfully maneuvered to replace Randolph with [Barnabas Bidwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnabas_Bidwell "Barnabas Bidwell") as floor leader.[[242]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson2002p49-258) This aroused suspicion of Jefferson and charges of undue executive influence over Congress. Jefferson signed the bill into law in February 1806. Six weeks later the law was made public. The two million dollars was to be given to France as payment, in turn, to put pressure on Spain to permit the annexation of Florida by the United States. France, however, refused the offer and Florida remained under Spanish control.[[242]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson2002p49-258) The failed venture damaged Jefferson's reputation among his supporters.[[242]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson2002p49-258)

#### _Chesapeake_–_Leopard_ affair

[![Image 21](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Leopardchesapeake.jpg/250px-Leopardchesapeake.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Leopardchesapeake.jpg)

An illustration of [HMS _Leopard_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Leopard "HMS Leopard") (right) firing on the [USS _Chesapeake_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Chesapeake "USS Chesapeake") in June 1807

Starting in 1806, the Royal Navy began stopping American merchantmen to search for deserters from the British navy; approximately 6,000 sailors were impressed into the Royal Navy this way, leading to deep anger and resentment among the U.S. public. In 1806, Jefferson issued a call for a boycott of British goods; on April 18, Congress passed the Non-Importation Acts, but they were never enforced. Later that year, Jefferson asked James Monroe and [William Pinkney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Pinkney "William Pinkney") to negotiate an end to foreign interference with American merchant shipping, though relations with Britain showed no signs of improving. The [Monroe–Pinkney Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe%E2%80%93Pinkney_Treaty "Monroe–Pinkney Treaty") was finalized but lacked any provisions regarding the issue of impressment, and Jefferson refused to submit it to the Senate for ratification.[[246]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Hayes505-262)

The British warship [HMS _Leopard_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Leopard_(1790) "HMS Leopard (1790)")[encountered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake%E2%80%93Leopard_affair "Chesapeake–Leopard affair") the [USS _Chesapeake_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Chesapeake_(1799) "USS Chesapeake (1799)") off the Virginia coast in June 1807; _Leopard_ fired at _Chesapeake_ after the latter refused to allow for a search for deserters before removing four deserters from the ship.[[247]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-embargo-263) Jefferson issued a proclamation banning British warships from U.S. waters. He presumed unilateral authority to call on the states to prepare 100,000 militia and ordered the purchase of arms, ammunition, and supplies, writing, "The laws of necessity, of self-preservation, of saving our country when in danger, are of higher obligation [than strict observance of written laws]". The [USS _Revenge_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Revenge_(1806) "USS Revenge (1806)") was dispatched to demand an explanation from the [British government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom "Government of the United Kingdom"), and Jefferson called for a special session of Congress in October to enact an embargo or alternatively to consider war.[[248]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-264)

#### Embargo (1807–1809)

[![Image 22: A turtle biting a man carrying a barrel to a waiting ship](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Ograbme.jpg/250px-Ograbme.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ograbme.jpg)

An 1807 political cartoon on the [Embargo Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo_Act_of_1807 "Embargo Act of 1807"), depicting merchants dodging the "Ograbme", which is "Embargo" spelled backward

In December 1807, news arrived that Napoleon had extended the [Berlin Decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Decree "Berlin Decree"), globally banning British imports. The Royal Navy, meanwhile continued to impress sailors from American merchant ships. However, Congress had no appetite to prepare the U.S. for war; Jefferson asked for and received the Embargo Act, an alternative that allowed the U.S. more time to build up defensive works, militias, and naval forces. Meacham argued that the [Embargo Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo_Act_of_1807 "Embargo Act of 1807") was a projection of power that surpassed the Alien and Sedition Acts, and R. B. Bernstein said that Jefferson "was pursuing policies resembling those he had cited in 1776 as grounds for independence and revolution".[[249]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-265)

In November 1807, Jefferson, for several days, met with his cabinet to discuss the deteriorating foreign situation.[[250]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-266) Secretary of State James Madison supported the embargo,[[251]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-267) while Treasury Secretary Gallatin opposed it, due to its indefinite time frame and the risk to the policy of American neutrality.[[252]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-268) The U.S. economy suffered, criticism grew, and opponents began evading the embargo. Instead of retreating, Jefferson sent federal agents to secretly track down smugglers and violators.[[253]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-269) Three acts were passed in Congress during 1807 and 1808, called the _Supplementary_, the _Additional_, and the _Enforcement_ acts.[[247]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-embargo-263) The government could not prevent American vessels from trading with the European belligerents once they had left American ports, although the embargo triggered a devastating decline in exports.[[247]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-embargo-263)

In December 1807, Jefferson announced his intention not to seek a third term. He turned his attention increasingly to Monticello during the last year of his presidency, giving Madison and Gallatin almost total control of affairs.[[254]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-270) Shortly before leaving office in March 1809, Jefferson signed the repeal of the Embargo. In its place, the [Non-Intercourse Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Intercourse_Act_(1809) "Non-Intercourse Act (1809)") was passed, but it proved no more effective.[[247]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-embargo-263) The day before Madison was inaugurated as his successor, Jefferson said that he felt like "a prisoner, released from his chains".[[255]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-271)

## Post-presidency (1809–1826)

After his presidency, Jefferson remained influential and continued to correspond with many of the country's leaders (including his two protégées, Madison and Monroe, who succeeded him as president); the [Monroe Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Doctrine "Monroe Doctrine") strongly resembles solicited advice that Jefferson gave to Monroe in 1823.[[256]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-272)[[257]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-273)

### University of Virginia

[![Image 23](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Lawn_UVa_Rotunda_Pavilions_II_and_IV.jpg/250px-Lawn_UVa_Rotunda_Pavilions_II_and_IV.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lawn_UVa_Rotunda_Pavilions_II_and_IV.jpg)

The [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia") in [Charlottesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia "Charlottesville, Virginia"), which Jefferson founded in January 1819

Jefferson envisioned a university free of church influences where students could specialize in new areas not offered at other colleges. He believed that education engendered a stable society, which should provide publicly funded schools accessible based solely on ability.[[258]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-274) He initially proposed his university in a letter to [Joseph Priestley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Priestley "Joseph Priestley") in 1800[[259]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-275) and, in 1819, founded the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia"). He organized the state legislative campaign for its charter and, with the assistance of [Edmund Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Bacon_(1785%E2%80%931866) "Edmund Bacon (1785–1866)"), purchased the location. He was the principal designer of the buildings, planned the university's curriculum, and served as the first rector upon its opening in 1825.[[260]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_1970_ch11-276)

Jefferson was a strong disciple of Greek and Roman architectural styles, which he believed to be most representative of American democracy. Each academic unit, called a pavilion, was designed with a two-story temple front, while the library "Rotunda" was modeled on the [Roman Pantheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome "Pantheon, Rome"). Jefferson referred to the university's grounds as the "[Academical Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lawn "The Lawn")", and he reflected his educational ideas in its layout. The ten pavilions included classrooms and faculty residences; they formed a quadrangle and were connected by colonnades, behind which stood the student rooms. Gardens and vegetable plots were placed behind the pavilions and were surrounded by [serpentine walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crinkle_crankle_wall "Crinkle crankle wall"), affirming the importance of the agrarian lifestyle.[[261]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-277) The university had a library rather than a church at its center, emphasizing its secular nature—controversial at the time.[[262]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-278)

When Jefferson died in 1826, James Madison replaced him as rector.[[263]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-279) Jefferson bequeathed most of his reconstructed library of almost 2,000 volumes to the university.[[264]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-280) Only one other ex-president has founded a university; [Millard Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millard_Fillmore "Millard Fillmore") founded the [University at Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo "University at Buffalo") in 1846.[[265]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-281)

### Reconciliation with Adams

[![Image 24](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Gilbert_Stuart%2C_Abigail_Smith_Adams_%28Mrs._John_Adams%29%2C_1800-1815%2C_NGA_42934.jpg/250px-Gilbert_Stuart%2C_Abigail_Smith_Adams_%28Mrs._John_Adams%29%2C_1800-1815%2C_NGA_42934.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gilbert_Stuart,_Abigail_Smith_Adams_(Mrs._John_Adams),_1800-1815,_NGA_42934.jpg)

In 1804, [Abigail Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abigail_Adams "Abigail Adams"), wife and confidant of [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"), was one of several people who intervened in an attempt to reconcile differences between Jefferson and John Adams. Jefferson and Adams ultimately reconciled, established a lengthy correspondence and renewed friendship, which historian [David McCullough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_McCullough "David McCullough") has called "one of the most extraordinary correspondences in American history." On July 4, 1826, the 50th anniversary of the adoption of the Declaration of Independence, Jefferson and Adams died within hours of each other, which then U.S. president [John Quincy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams "John Quincy Adams") called "visible and palpable remarks of Divine Favor."

Jefferson and [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") became good friends in the first decades of their political careers, serving together in the Continental Congress in the 1770s and in Europe in the 1780s. The Federalist/Republican split of the 1790s divided them, however, and Adams felt betrayed by Jefferson's sponsorship of partisan attacks, such as those of James Callender. Jefferson was angered by Adams' appointment of "midnight judges".[[266]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Freeman2008-282) The two men did not communicate directly for more than a decade after Jefferson succeeded Adams as president.[[267]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-283) A brief correspondence took place between [Abigail Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abigail_Adams "Abigail Adams") and Jefferson after Jefferson's daughter Polly died in 1804, in an attempt at reconciliation unknown to Adams. However, an exchange of letters resumed open hostilities between Adams and Jefferson.[[266]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Freeman2008-282)

As early as 1809, [Benjamin Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Rush "Benjamin Rush") began to prod the two through correspondence to re-establish contact.[[266]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Freeman2008-282) In 1812, Adams wrote a short New Year's greeting to Jefferson, prompted earlier by Rush, to which Jefferson warmly responded. This initial correspondence began what historian [David McCullough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_McCullough "David McCullough") calls "one of the most extraordinary correspondences in American history".[[268]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-284) Over the next 14 years, Jefferson and Adams exchanged 158 letters discussing their political differences, justifying their respective roles in events, and debating the revolution's import to the world.[[269]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-285)

When Adams died on July 4, 1826, the 50th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence, his last words were an acknowledgment of his longtime friend and rival. "Thomas Jefferson survives", Adams said, unaware that Jefferson had died a few hours earlier.[[270]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-286)[[271]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-287)[[272]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-288)

### Autobiography

In 1821, at the age of 77, Jefferson began writing his _Autobiography of Thomas Jefferson: 1743–1790_, in which he said he sought to "state some recollections of dates and facts concerning myself".[[273]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Jeffbio-289) He focused on the struggles and achievements he experienced until July 29, 1790, where the narrative stopped short.[[274]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-290) He excluded his youth, emphasizing the revolutionary era. He related that his ancestors came from Wales to America in the early 17th century and settled in the western frontier of the Virginia colony, which influenced his zeal for individual and state rights. Jefferson described his father as uneducated, but with a "strong mind and sound judgement". He also addressed his enrollment in the [College of William & Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_William_%26_Mary "College of William & Mary") and his election to the Continental Congress in Philadelphia in 1775.[[273]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Jeffbio-289)

He expressed opposition to the idea of a privileged [aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy "Aristocracy") made up of large landowning families partial to the King, and instead promoted "the aristocracy of virtue and talent, which nature has wisely provided for the direction of the interests of society, & scattered with equal hand through all its conditions, was deemed essential to a well-ordered republic".[[273]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Jeffbio-289) The work is primarily concerned with the Declaration and reforming the government of Virginia. He used notes, letters, and documents to tell many of the stories. He suggested that this history was so rich that his personal affairs were better overlooked, but he incorporated a self-analysis using the Declaration and other patriotism.[[275]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-291)

### Greek War of Independence

Thomas Jefferson was a [philhellene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philhellenism "Philhellenism"), lover of Greek culture, who sympathized with the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence "Greek War of Independence").[[276]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaufmanMacpherson2005427-292)[[277]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201717-293) He has been described as the most influential of the [Founding Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States "Founding Fathers of the United States") who supported the Greek cause,[[277]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201717-293)[[278]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEarle192749-294) viewing it as similar to the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution").[[279]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201729-295) By 1823, Jefferson was exchanging ideas with Greek scholar [Adamantios Korais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamantios_Korais "Adamantios Korais").[[277]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201717-293) Jefferson advised Korais on building the political system of [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece") by using [classical liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_liberalism "Classical liberalism") and examples from the American governmental system, ultimately prescribing a government akin to that of a U.S. state.[[280]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201718-296) He also suggested the application of a [classical education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_education_movement "Classical education movement") for the newly founded [First Hellenic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hellenic_Republic "First Hellenic Republic").[[281]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201719-297) Jefferson's philosophical instructions were welcomed by the [Greek people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_people "Greek people").[[281]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201719-297) Korais became one of the designers of the [Greek constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_constitution "Greek constitution") and urged his associates to study Jefferson's works and other literature from the American Revolution.[[281]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacavone201719-297)

### Lafayette's visit

[![Image 25](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Lafayette-scheffer.jpg/250px-Lafayette-scheffer.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lafayette-scheffer.jpg)

An 1824 portrait of the [Marquis de Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette "Marquis de Lafayette") by [Ary Scheffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ary_Scheffer "Ary Scheffer")

In the summer of 1824, the [Marquis de Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette "Marquis de Lafayette") accepted an invitation from President [James Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Monroe "James Monroe") to visit the country. Jefferson and Lafayette had not seen each other since 1789. After visits to New York, [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England "New England"), and Washington, Lafayette arrived at [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello") on November 4.[[260]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_1970_ch11-276)

Jefferson's grandson [Randolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_Randolph "Thomas Jefferson Randolph") was present and recorded the reunion: "As they approached each other, their uncertain gait quickened itself into a shuffling run, and exclaiming, 'Ah Jefferson!' 'Ah Lafayette!', they burst into tears as they fell into each other's arms." Jefferson and Lafayette then retired to the house to reminisce.[[282]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-298) The next morning Jefferson, Lafayette, and James Madison attended a tour and banquet at the University of Virginia. Jefferson had someone else read a speech he had prepared for Lafayette, as his voice was weak and could not carry. This was his last public presentation. After an 11-day visit, Lafayette bid Jefferson goodbye and departed Monticello.[[283]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-299)

### Final days, death, and burial

[![Image 26: Obelisk at Thomas Jefferson's gravesite](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Thomas_Jefferson%27s_Grave_Site.jpg/250px-Thomas_Jefferson%27s_Grave_Site.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Thomas_Jefferson%27s_Grave_Site.jpg)

Jefferson's gravesite at [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello")

Jefferson's approximately $100,000 of debt weighed heavily on his mind in his final months, as it became increasingly clear that he would have little to leave to his heirs.[[284]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-300)[[285]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-301) In February 1826, he successfully applied to the General Assembly to hold a public lottery as a fundraiser.[[286]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ellis288-302) His health began to deteriorate in July 1825, due to a combination of [rheumatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheumatism "Rheumatism") from arm and wrist injuries, and [intestinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract") and [urinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urology "Urology") disorders.[[260]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_1970_ch11-276) By June 1826, he was confined to bed.[[286]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ellis288-302) On July 3, overcome by fever, Jefferson declined an invitation to attend an anniversary celebration of the Declaration in [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.").[[287]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-303)

During his last hours, he was accompanied by family members and friends. Jefferson died on July 4, 1826, at 12:50 p.m. at age 83, on the 50th anniversary of the adoption of the Declaration of Independence. In the moments prior to his death, Jefferson instructed his treating physician, "No, doctor, nothing more", refusing [laudanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laudanum "Laudanum"). But his final significant words were, "Is it the Fourth?" or "This is the Fourth".[[288]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-304) When his predecessor, [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"), died at approximately 6:20 pm that same day, his last words were "Thomas Jefferson survives", though Adams was unaware that Jefferson had died several hours before.[[289]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-305)[[290]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-306)[[291]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-rayner428%E2%80%9329-307)[[292]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Berstein189-308) The sitting president was Adams's son, [John Quincy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams "John Quincy Adams"), and he called the coincidence of their deaths on the nation's anniversary "visible and palpable remarks of Divine Favor".[[293]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-309)

Shortly after Jefferson died, attendants found a gold locket on a chain around his neck, containing a small faded blue ribbon around a lock of his wife [Martha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Jefferson "Martha Jefferson")'s hair.[[294]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-310)

Jefferson was interred at [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello"), under an [epitaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epitaph "Epitaph") that he wrote:

> HERE WAS BURIED THOMAS JEFFERSON, AUTHOR OF THE DECLARATION OF AMERICAN INDEPENDENCE, OF THE STATUTE OF VIRGINIA FOR RELIGIOUS FREEDOM, AND FATHER OF THE UNIVERSITY OF VIRGINIA.[[295]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-311)

In his advanced years, Jefferson became increasingly concerned that people would understand the principles in the Declaration of Independence, and the people responsible for writing it, and he continually defended himself as its author. He considered the document one of his greatest life achievements, in addition to authoring the [Statute of Virginia for Religious Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Virginia_for_Religious_Freedom "Statute of Virginia for Religious Freedom") and founding the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia"). Absent from his epitaph were his political roles, including his presidency.[[296]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-312)

Jefferson died deeply in debt, and was unable to pass on his estate freely to his heirs.[[297]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-313) He gave instructions in his will for disposal of his assets,[[298]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-314) including the freeing of Sally Hemings's children;[[299]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham,_2012,_p._495-315) but his estate, possessions, and slaves were sold at public auctions starting in 1827.[[300]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-316) In 1831, Monticello was sold by [Martha Jefferson Randolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Jefferson_Randolph "Martha Jefferson Randolph") and the other heirs.[[301]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-317)

## Political, social, and religious views

Jefferson subscribed to the political ideals expounded by [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke "John Locke"), [Francis Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Bacon "Francis Bacon"), and [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton"), whom he considered the three greatest men who ever lived.[[302]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Hayes,_2008,_p._10-318)[[303]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cogliano_p._14-319) He was also influenced by the writings of [Gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Gibbon "Edward Gibbon"), [Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume "David Hume"), [Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Robertson_(historian) "William Robertson (historian)"), [Bolingbroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_St_John,_1st_Viscount_Bolingbroke "Henry St John, 1st Viscount Bolingbroke"), [Montesquieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montesquieu "Montesquieu"), and [Voltaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaire "Voltaire").[[304]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cogliano_p._26-320) Jefferson thought that the independent [yeoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeoman#United_States "Yeoman") and agrarian life were ideals of [republican virtues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_the_United_States "Republicanism in the United States"). He distrusted cities and financiers, favored decentralized government power, and believed that the tyranny that had plagued the common man in Europe was due to corrupt political establishments and monarchies. He supported efforts to disestablish the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England"),[[305]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-321) wrote the [Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Statute_for_Religious_Freedom "Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom"), and he pressed for a [wall of separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state_in_the_United_States "Separation of church and state in the United States") between church and state.[[306]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-322) The Republicans under Jefferson were strongly influenced by the 18th-century British [Whig Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_(British_political_party) "Whig (British political party)"), which believed in [limited government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_government "Limited government").[[307]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-323) His [Democratic-Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-Republican_Party "Democratic-Republican Party") became dominant in [early American politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Party_System "First Party System"), and his views became known as [Jeffersonian democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffersonian_democracy "Jeffersonian democracy").[[308]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-324)[[309]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Smith2003_p_314-325)

### Philosophy, society, and government

Jefferson wrote letters and speeches prolifically; these show him to be well-read in the philosophical literature of his day and of antiquity. Nevertheless, some scholars do not take Jefferson seriously as a philosopher mainly because he did not produce a formal work on philosophy. However, he has been described as one of the most outstanding philosophical figures of his time because his work provided the theoretical background to, and the substance of, the social and political events of the revolutionary years and the development of the [American Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Constitution "American Constitution") in the 1770s and 1780s.[[310]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-326) Jefferson continued to attend to more theoretical questions of [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy "Natural philosophy") and subsequently left behind a rich philosophical legacy in the form of presidential messages, letters, and public papers.[[311]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-327)

Jefferson described himself as an [Epicurean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicureanism "Epicureanism") and agreed with [Epictetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epictetus "Epictetus")' works.[[312]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-328)[[313]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Richard-329) Jefferson knew Epicurean philosophy from original sources, but also mentioned [Pierre Gassendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gassendi "Pierre Gassendi")'s _[Syntagma philosophicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre\_Gassendi#Syntagma\_philosophicum "Pierre Gassendi")_ as influencing his ideas on Epicureanism.[[314]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-330)

According to Jefferson's philosophy, citizens have "certain inalienable rights" and "rightful liberty is unobstructed action according to our will, within limits drawn around us by the equal rights of others".[[315]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-331)[[316]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-De_Witte-332) A staunch advocate of the jury system, he proclaimed in 1801, "I consider [trial by jury] as the only anchor yet imagined by man, by which a government can be held to the principles of its constitution."[[317]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-333) Jeffersonian government not only prohibited individuals in society from infringing on the liberty of others, but also restrained itself from diminishing [individual liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_liberty "Individual liberty") as a protection against [tyranny of the majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyranny_of_the_majority "Tyranny of the majority").[[318]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-334) Initially, Jefferson favored restricted voting to those who could actually have the free exercise of their reason by escaping any corrupting dependence on others. He advocated enfranchising a majority of Virginians, seeking to expand suffrage to include "yeoman farmers" who owned their own land while excluding tenant farmers, city day laborers, vagrants, most American Indians, and women.[[319]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Wood220-335)

He was convinced that individual liberties were the fruit of political equality, which was threatened by the arbitrary government.[[320]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-336) Excesses of democracy in his view were caused by institutional corruption rather than human nature. He was less suspicious of a working democracy than many contemporaries.[[319]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Wood220-335) As president, Jefferson feared that the [federal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_the_United_States "Federalism in the United States") enacted by Washington and Adams had encouraged corrupting patronage and dependence. He tried to restore a balance between the state and federal governments more nearly reflecting the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation "Articles of Confederation"), seeking to reinforce state prerogatives where his party was in the majority.[[319]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Wood220-335)

According to Stanford Scholar [Jack Rakove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_N._Rakove "Jack N. Rakove"), "[w]hen Jefferson wrote 'all men are created equal' in the preamble to the Declaration, he was not talking about individual equality. What he really meant was that the American colonists, as a people, had the same rights of self-government as other peoples, and hence could declare independence, create new governments and assume their 'separate and equal station' among other nations."[[316]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-De_Witte-332) Jefferson's famous mantra later became a statement "of individual equality that everyone and every member of a deprived group could claim for himself or herself".[[316]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-De_Witte-332) Historian [Henry Wiencek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wiencek "Henry Wiencek") has noted Jefferson included slaves when he penned "_all men are created equal_" in the Declaration. As early as 1774, Jefferson had supported ending domestic slavery, and making slaves citizens.[[321]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-337) Later, writing in _Notes_ (1781), Jefferson supported gradual emancipation of slaves, to be sent away from the U.S. to an unspecified place. The former slaves would be replaced by white immigrant workers.[[322]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-338) In 1792, Jefferson calculated that he was making a 4 percent profit every year on the birth of black children. After this he wrote that slavery presented an investment strategy for the future. Historian Brion Davis writes that Jefferson's emancipation efforts virtually ceased.[[323]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-339)

Jefferson was steeped in the [Whig tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiggism "Whiggism") of the oppressed majority set against a repeatedly unresponsive court party in the Parliament. He justified small outbreaks of rebellion as necessary to get monarchial regimes to amend oppressive measures compromising popular liberties. In a republican regime ruled by the majority, he acknowledged "it will often be exercised when wrong".[[324]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-340) But "the remedy is to set them right as to facts, pardon and pacify them".[[325]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-341) As Jefferson saw his party triumph in two terms of his presidency and launch into a third term under James Madison, his view of the U.S. as a continental republic and an "empire of liberty" grew more upbeat. On departing the presidency, he described America as "trusted with the destines of this solitary republic of the world, the only monument of human rights, and the sole depository of the sacred fire of freedom and self-government".[[326]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-342)

Jefferson was a supporter of [American expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_expansionism "American expansionism"), writing in 1801 that "it is impossible not to look forward to distant times when our rapid multiplication will expand itself beyond those limits, and cover the whole northern, if not the southern continent".[[327]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-343)

### Democracy

[![Image 27: Elder Jefferson](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Jefferson_Portrait_West_Point_by_Thomas_Sully.jpg/250px-Jefferson_Portrait_West_Point_by_Thomas_Sully.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jefferson_Portrait_West_Point_by_Thomas_Sully.jpg)

Jefferson, at age 78, depicted in an 1821 [Thomas Sully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Sully "Thomas Sully") portrait

Jefferson considered democracy to be the expression of society and promoted national self-determination, cultural uniformity, and education of all males of the commonwealth.[[328]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-344) He supported public education and a free press as essential components of a democratic nation.[[329]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-345)

After resigning as [secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State "United States Secretary of State") in 1793, Jefferson focused on the electoral bases of the Republicans and Federalists. The "Republican" classification for which he advocated included "the entire body of landholders" everywhere and "the body of laborers" without land.[[330]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-346) Republicans united behind Jefferson as vice president, with the election of 1796 expanding democracy nationwide at grassroots levels.[[331]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-347) Jefferson promoted Republican candidates for local offices.[[332]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-348)

Beginning with Jefferson's electioneering for the "revolution of 1800", his political efforts were based on egalitarian appeals.[[333]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-349) In his later years, he referred to the 1800 election "as real a revolution in the principles of our government as that of '76 was in its form", one "not effected indeed by the sword... but by the... suffrage of the people".[[334]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-350) Voter participation grew during Jefferson's presidency, increasing to "unimaginable levels" compared to the Federalist Era, with turnout of about 67,000 in [1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_United_States_presidential_election "1800 United States presidential election") rising to about 143,000 in [1804](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1804_United_States_presidential_election "1804 United States presidential election").[[335]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-351)

At the onset of the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution"), Jefferson accepted [William Blackstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Blackstone "William Blackstone")'s argument that property ownership would sufficiently empower voters' independent judgement, but he sought to further expand suffrage by land distribution to the poor.[[336]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-352) In the heat of the Revolutionary Era and afterward, several states expanded voter eligibility from landed gentry to all propertied male, tax-paying citizens with Jefferson's support.[[337]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-353) In retirement, he gradually became critical of his home state for violating "the principle of equal political rights"—the social right of universal male suffrage.[[338]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-354) He sought a "general suffrage" of all taxpayers and militia-men, and equal representation by population in the General Assembly to correct preferential treatment of the slave-holding regions.[[339]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-355)

### Religion

[![Image 28: A leather-bound Bible](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Thomas_Jefferson_Bible_Lined_Cover.jpg/250px-Thomas_Jefferson_Bible_Lined_Cover.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Thomas_Jefferson_Bible_Lined_Cover.jpg)

The _[Jefferson Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson\_Bible "Jefferson Bible")_, which features only the words of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus") from his disciples, written in parallel [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language"), [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"), and English

Baptized in his youth, Jefferson became a governing member of his local [Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States) "Episcopal Church (United States)") in [Charlottesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia "Charlottesville, Virginia"), which he later attended with his daughters.[[340]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-356) Jefferson, however, spurned Biblical views of Christianity.[[341]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cunningham_2020-357) Influenced by [Deist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deism "Deism") authors during his college years, Jefferson abandoned mainstream Christianity after his review of [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament "New Testament") teachings.[[342]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-358)[[343]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-359) Jefferson has sometimes been portrayed as a follower of the liberal religious strand of Deism that values reason over revelation.[[344]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-:1a-360) Nonetheless, in 1803, Jefferson asserted, "I am Christian, in the only sense in which [Jesus] wished any one to be".[[219]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Randal583-233)

Later, influenced by prominent [Unitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarianism "Unitarianism") theologist, [Joseph Priestley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Priestley "Joseph Priestley"),[[344]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-:1a-360) Jefferson selected New Testament passages of Jesus' teachings into a private work he called _The Life and Morals of Jesus of Nazareth_, known today as the _[Jefferson Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson\_Bible "Jefferson Bible")_, which was never published during his lifetime.[[345]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Jefferson_Bible,_1820-361)[[346]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-ReferenceB-362) Jefferson believed that Jesus' message had been obscured and corrupted by [Paul the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle "Paul the Apostle"), the [Gospel writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Evangelists "Four Evangelists") and [Protestant reformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformers "Protestant Reformers").[[344]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-:1a-360) Peterson states that Jefferson was a [theist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theism "Theism") "whose God was the Creator of the universe... all the evidences of nature testified to His perfection; and man could rely on the harmony and beneficence of His work".[[347]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-363) In a letter to [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams"), Jefferson wrote that what he believed was genuinely Christ's, found in the Gospels, was "as easily distinguishable as diamonds in a dunghill".[[341]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cunningham_2020-357) By omitting miracles and the [resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus "Resurrection of Jesus"), Jefferson made the figure of Jesus more compatible with a worldview based on reason,[[341]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cunningham_2020-357) and defined being a Christian as one who followed the simple teachings of Jesus.

Jefferson was firmly [anticlerical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clericalism "Anti-clericalism"), writing in "every age, the priest has been hostile to liberty... they have perverted the purest religion ever preached to man into mystery and jargon."[[348]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-364) The full letter to Horatio Spatford can be read at the National Archives.[[349]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-365) Jefferson once supported banning clergy from public office but later relented.[[350]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-366) In 1777, he drafted the [Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Statute_for_Religious_Freedom "Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom"). Ratified in 1786, it made compelling attendance or contributions to any state-sanctioned religious establishment illegal and declared that men "shall be free to profess... their opinions in matters of religion".[[351]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-367) The Statute is one of only three accomplishments he chose for his epitaph.[[352]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-368)[[353]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-369) Early in 1802, Jefferson wrote to the Danbury Connecticut Baptist Association that "religion is a matter which lies solely between Man and his God". He interpreted the [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution") as having built "a wall of [separation between Church and State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state "Separation of church and state")".[[354]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-370) The phrase 'Separation of Church and State' has been cited several times by the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") in its interpretation of the [Establishment Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Establishment_Clause "Establishment Clause").[[355]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-371)

Jefferson donated to the [American Bible Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bible_Society "American Bible Society"), saying the [Four Evangelists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Evangelists "Four Evangelists") delivered a "pure and sublime system of morality" to humanity. He thought Americans would rationally create "[Apiarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beekeeping "Beekeeping")" religion, extracting the best traditions of every denomination.[[356]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-372) He contributed generously to several local denominations near Monticello.[[357]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-373) Acknowledging [organized religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_religion "Organized religion") would always be factored into political life, he encouraged reason over supernatural revelation to make inquiries into religion. He believed in a [creator god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity "Creator deity"), an [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife "Afterlife"), and the sum of religion as loving God and neighbors. But he also controversially rejected fundamental Christian beliefs, denying the conventional Christian [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity "Trinity"), Jesus's divinity as the [Son of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_of_God "Son of God") and miracles, the Resurrection of Christ, atonement from sin, and [original sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_sin "Original sin").[[346]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-ReferenceB-362)[[358]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham_pp._471-374)[[359]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Sanford_pp._85-375) Jefferson believed that original sin was a gross injustice.[[346]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-ReferenceB-362)

Jefferson's unorthodox religious beliefs became an important issue in the [1800 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_United_States_presidential_election "1800 United States presidential election").[[360]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Wood586-376) Federalists attacked him as an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist"). As president, Jefferson countered the accusations by praising religion in his inaugural address and attending services at the Capitol.[[360]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Wood586-376) Jefferson broadly agreed with the [Unitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarianism "Unitarianism") view on Christianity, believing in one [creator god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity "Creator deity"), but denying the [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity "Trinity") and resurrection.[[346]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-ReferenceB-362)[[358]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham_pp._471-374)[[359]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Sanford_pp._85-375) Further, in 1822, Jefferson wrote, "I confidently expect that the present generation will see Unitarianism become the general religion of the United States."[[361]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Jeff-Smith-1822-377)

### Banks

[![Image 29](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Alexander_Hamilton_%28NPG%29.jpg/250px-Alexander_Hamilton_%28NPG%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alexander_Hamilton_(NPG).jpg)

Jefferson opposed Treasury Secretary [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton "Alexander Hamilton")'s proposal to establish a government bank, and the two emerged as political rivals during [George Washington's presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_Washington "Presidency of George Washington").

Jefferson distrusted government banks and opposed public borrowing, which he thought created long-term debt, bred monopolies, and invited dangerous speculation as opposed to productive labor.[[362]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-378) In one letter to Madison, he argued each generation should curtail all debt within 19 years, and not impose a long-term debt on subsequent generations.[[363]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-379)

In another letter to John Taylor, Jefferson wrote,[[364]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-380)

> Banking establishments are more dangerous than standing armies; & that the principle of spending money to be paid by posterity, under the name of funding, is but swindling futurity on a large scale.

In 1791, President Washington asked Jefferson, then secretary of state, and Hamilton, the secretary of the treasury, if the Congress had the authority to create a [national bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bank_of_the_United_States "First Bank of the United States"). While Hamilton believed so, Jefferson and Madison thought a national bank would ignore the needs of individuals and farmers, and would violate the [Tenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Tenth Amendment to the United States Constitution") by assuming powers not granted to the federal government by the states.[[365]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-381) Hamilton successfully argued that the [implied powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implied_powers "Implied powers") given to the federal government in the Constitution supported the creation of a national bank, among other federal actions.

Jefferson used agrarian resistance to banks and speculators as the first defining principle of an opposition party, recruiting candidates for Congress on the issue as early as 1792.[[366]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-382) As president, Jefferson was persuaded by Secretary of the Treasury Albert Gallatin to leave the bank intact but sought to restrain its influence.[[367]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-383)[[q]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-385)

### Slavery

[![Image 30: Farm Book page](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Jefferson_slaves.jpg/250px-Jefferson_slaves.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jefferson_slaves.jpg)

Page 30 of Jefferson's 1795 Farm Book, which lists 163 slaves at Monticello

Scholars give radically differing interpretations on [Jefferson's views and relationship with slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_and_slavery "Thomas Jefferson and slavery").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cogliano-2008-6) Opinions range from "emancipationists" who view him as an early [proto-abolitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism "Abolitionism"), who subsequently made pragmatic compromises with the [slave power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_Power "Slave Power") to [preserve the union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_in_the_United_States "Secession in the United States"); to "[revisionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_revisionism "Historical revisionism")", who argue that he in fact entrenched the institution in American society; with people also having more nuanced opinions, who either argue that Jefferson held inconsistent views on the institution throughout his lifetime or that both interpretations are too overly simplistic.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Cogliano-2008-6)

Jefferson lived in a planter economy largely dependent upon slavery, and as a wealthy landholder, used slave labor for his household, plantation, and workshops. He first recorded his slaveholding in 1774, when he counted 41 enslaved people.[[369]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-386) Over his lifetime he enslaved about 600 people; he inherited about 175 people while most of the remainder were people born on his plantations.[[370]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFSlaveryFAQ-387) Jefferson purchased some slaves in order to reunite their families. He sold approximately 110 people for economic reasons, primarily slaves from his outlying farms.[[370]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFSlaveryFAQ-387)[[371]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-388) In 1784, when the number of people he enslaved likely was approximately 200, he began to divest himself of many slaves, and by 1794 he had divested himself of 161 individuals.[[372]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-PBS_Stanton-389)[[r]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-390)

Approximately 100 slaves lived at Monticello at any given time. In 1817, the plantation recorded its largest slave population of 140 individuals.[[373]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-391)

Jefferson once said, "My first wish is that the labourers may be well treated".[[370]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFSlaveryFAQ-387) Jefferson did not work his slaves on Sundays and Christmas and he allowed them more personal time during the winter months.[[374]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-392) Some scholars doubt Jefferson's benevolence,[[375]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-393) noting cases of excessive slave whippings in his absence. His nail factory was staffed only by enslaved children. Many of the enslaved boys became tradesmen. Burwell Colbert, who started his working life as a child in Monticello's Nailery, was later promoted to the supervisory position of butler.[[376]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-394)

Jefferson felt slavery was harmful to both slave and master but had reservations about releasing slaves from captivity, and advocated for gradual emancipation.[[377]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFslavery-395)[[378]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-396)[[379]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-397) In 1779, he proposed gradual voluntary training and resettlement to the Virginia legislature, and three years later drafted legislation allowing slaveholders to free their own slaves.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Meacham,_2012,_p._105-71) In his draft of the Declaration of Independence, he included a section, stricken by other Southern delegates, criticizing King George III for supposedly forcing slavery onto the colonies.[[380]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-398) In 1784, Jefferson proposed the abolition of slavery in all western U.S. territories, limiting slave importation to 15 years.[[381]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ferling_2000_p287-399) Congress, however, failed to pass his proposal by one vote.[[381]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ferling_2000_p287-399) In 1787, Congress passed the Northwest Ordinance, a partial victory for Jefferson that terminated slavery in the Northwest Territory. Jefferson freed his slave Robert Hemings in 1794 and he freed his cook slave James Hemings in 1796.[[382]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-400) Jefferson freed his runaway slave [Harriet Hemings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Hemings "Harriet Hemings") in 1822. Upon his death in 1826, Jefferson freed five male Hemings slaves in his will.[[383]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-401)

During his presidency, Jefferson allowed the diffusion of slavery into the [Louisiana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Territory "Louisiana Territory") hoping to prevent slave uprisings in Virginia and to prevent [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina "South Carolina") secession.[[384]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-402) In 1804, in a compromise, Jefferson and Congress banned domestic slave trafficking for one year into the Louisiana Territory.[[385]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-403) In 1806 he officially called for anti-slavery legislation terminating the import or export of slaves. Congress passed the law in 1807.[[377]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFslavery-395)[[386]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-404)[[387]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ferling_2000_p288-405)

In 1819, Jefferson strongly opposed a Missouri statehood application amendment, which banned domestic slave importation and freed slaves at the age of 25 on grounds that it would destroy the union.[[388]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ferling_2000_pp286_294-406) In _Notes on the State of Virginia_, he created controversy by calling slavery a moral evil for which the nation would ultimately have to account to God.[[389]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-407) Jefferson wrote of his "suspicion" that Black people were mentally and physically inferior to Whites, but argued that they nonetheless had innate human rights.[[377]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFslavery-395)[[390]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-408)[[391]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-409) He therefore supported colonization plans that would transport freed slaves to another country, such as [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia "Liberia") or [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone"), though he recognized the impracticability of such proposals.[[392]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-410) According to [Eric Foner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Foner "Eric Foner"), "In 1824 Jefferson proposed that the federal government purchase and deport 'the increase of each year' (that is, children), so that the slave population would age and eventually disappear."[[393]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-411)

During his presidency, Jefferson was for the most part publicly silent on the issue of slavery and emancipation,[[394]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFAntiSlaveryActions-412) as the Congressional debate over slavery and its extension caused a dangerous north–south rift among the states, with talk of a northern confederacy in New England.[[395]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-413)[[s]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-414) The violent attacks on white slave owners during the [Haitian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution "Haitian Revolution") due to injustices under slavery supported Jefferson's fears of a race war, increasing his reservations about promoting emancipation.[[377]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFslavery-395)[[396]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-415) After numerous attempts and failures to bring about emancipation,[[397]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-416) Jefferson wrote privately in an 1805 letter to [William A. Burwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_A._Burwell "William A. Burwell"), "I have long since given up the expectation of any early provision for the extinguishment of slavery among us." That same year he also related this idea to [George Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Logan_(Pennsylvania_politician) "George Logan (Pennsylvania politician)"), writing, "I have most carefully avoided every public act or manifestation on that subject."[[398]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-417)

#### Jefferson–Hemings controversy

[![Image 31](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Cock_ca1804_attrib_to_JamesAkin_AmericanAntiquarianSociety.png/250px-Cock_ca1804_attrib_to_JamesAkin_AmericanAntiquarianSociety.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cock_ca1804_attrib_to_JamesAkin_AmericanAntiquarianSociety.png)

An 1804 cartoon depicting Jefferson as a rooster and [Sally Hemings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Hemings "Sally Hemings") as a hen

Claims that Jefferson fathered children with his slave Sally Hemings after his wife's death have been debated since 1802. In that year [James T. Callender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_T._Callender "James T. Callender"), after being denied a position as [postmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmaster "Postmaster"), alleged Jefferson had taken Hemings as a concubine and fathered several children with her.[[399]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-418) In 1998, a panel of researchers conducted a [Y-DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-DNA "Y-DNA") study of living descendants of Jefferson's uncle, Field, and of a descendant of Hemings's son, [Eston Hemings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eston_Hemings "Eston Hemings"). The results showed a match with the male Jefferson line.[[400]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-419)[[401]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas_Jefferson_and_Sally_Hemings_A_Brief_Account-420) Subsequently, the Thomas Jefferson Foundation (TJF) formed a nine-member research team of historians to assess the matter.[[401]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas_Jefferson_and_Sally_Hemings_A_Brief_Account-420) The TJF report concluded that "the DNA study... indicates a high probability that Thomas Jefferson fathered Eston Hemings".[[401]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas_Jefferson_and_Sally_Hemings_A_Brief_Account-420)[[402]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-421)[[t]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-423) The TJF also concluded that Jefferson likely fathered all of Hemings's children listed at Monticello.[[401]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas_Jefferson_and_Sally_Hemings_A_Brief_Account-420)[[u]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-424)

In July 2017, the TJF announced that archeological excavations at Monticello had revealed what they believe to have been Sally Hemings's quarters, adjacent to Jefferson's bedroom.[[404]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-nbc-425)[[405]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-wapost-426) Since the results of the DNA tests were made public, the consensus among most historians has been that Jefferson had a sexual relationship with Sally Hemings and that he was the father of her son Eston Hemings.[[406]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-427)

A minority of scholars maintain the evidence is insufficient to prove Jefferson's paternity conclusively. Based on DNA and other evidence, they note the possibility that additional Jefferson males, including his brother Randolph Jefferson and any one of Randolph's four sons, or his cousin, could have fathered Sally Hemings's children.[[407]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-428) In 2002, historian [Merrill Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrill_Peterson "Merrill Peterson") said: "in the absence of direct documentary evidence either proving or refuting the allegation, nothing conclusive can be said about Jefferson's relations with Sally Hemings."[[408]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_2002_p._43-429) Concerning the 1998 DNA study, Peterson said that "the results of the DNA testing of Jefferson and Hemings descendants provided support for the idea that Jefferson was the father of at least one of Sally Hemings's children".[[408]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Peterson_2002_p._43-429)

After Jefferson's death in 1826, although not formally [manumitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manumission#United_States "Manumission"), Sally Hemings was allowed by Jefferson's daughter Martha to live in [Charlottesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia "Charlottesville, Virginia") as a [free woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_negro "Free negro") with her two sons until her death in 1835.[[409]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-430)[[v]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-432) The [Monticello Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello_Association "Monticello Association") refused to allow Sally Hemings' descendants the right of burial at Monticello.[[411]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-FOOTNOTECBSNews2019-433)

## Interests and activities

[![Image 32](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/MJK50147_Virginia_State_Capitol.jpg/250px-MJK50147_Virginia_State_Capitol.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MJK50147_Virginia_State_Capitol.jpg)

[Virginia State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Capitol "Virginia State Capitol") in [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia "Richmond, Virginia"), which Jefferson designed

Jefferson was a farmer, obsessed with new crops, soil conditions, garden designs, and scientific agricultural techniques. His main cash crop was tobacco, but its price was usually low and it was rarely profitable. He tried to achieve self-sufficiency with wheat, vegetables, flax, corn, hogs, sheep, poultry, and cattle, but he lived perpetually beyond his means[[412]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-434) and was always in debt.[[413]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-435) Jefferson also planted two vineyards at Monticello and hoped to grow _Vitis vinifera,_ the European wine grape species, to make wine, but the crop failed. His efforts were nonetheless an important contribution to the development of American viticulture.[[414]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-436)

Jefferson mastered architecture through [self-study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodidacticism "Autodidacticism"). His primary authority was [Andrea Palladio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Palladio "Andrea Palladio")'s 1570 _[The Four Books of Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Four\_Books\_of\_Architecture "The Four Books of Architecture")_.[[415]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-437) Jefferson helped popularize the Neo-[Palladian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladian_architecture "Palladian architecture") style in the United States, utilizing designs for the [Virginia State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Capitol "Virginia State Capitol"), the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia"), [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello"), and others.[[416]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-438)

In [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology "Archaeology") in 1784, Jefferson, using the [trench method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench "Trench"), started excavating a [Monacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monacan_Indian_Nation "Monacan Indian Nation")[[417]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-439)[burial mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial_mound "Burial mound") in [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia "Virginia"). His excavations were prompted by him noticing local Native Americans visiting the site and the ["Moundbuilders" question.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mound_Builders "Mound Builders")[[418]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-440) His methods allowed him to witness the [stratigraphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratigraphy "Stratigraphy") layout, the various human remains and other artifacts inside the mound. The evidence present at the site led him to admit that he saw no reason why the ancestors of the present-day Native Americans could not have raised those mounds.[[419]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-441)

He was interested in birds and wine, and was a noted [gourmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourmet "Gourmet").[[420]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Hayes135-442) As a naturalist, he was fascinated by the [Natural Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Bridge_(Virginia) "Natural Bridge (Virginia)") geological formation, and in 1774 successfully acquired the Bridge by a grant from George III.[[421]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-443) As an advocate of Enlightenment ideals, Jefferson studied many aspects of the natural sciences and frequently corresponded, and even hosted on multiple occasions, with [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia") explorer, [Alexander von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt "Alexander von Humboldt").[[422]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-444)

### American Philosophical Society

Jefferson was a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") for 35 years, beginning in 1780. Through the society he advanced the [sciences and Enlightenment ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_in_the_Age_of_Enlightenment "Science in the Age of Enlightenment"), emphasizing that knowledge of science reinforced and extended freedom.[[423]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Hayes432-445) His _Notes on the State of Virginia_ was written in part as a contribution to the society.[[424]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFAPS-446) He became the society's third president on March 3, 1797, a few months after he was elected Vice President of the United States.[[424]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFAPS-446)[[425]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Berstein118-447)

On March 10, 1797, Jefferson gave a lecture, later published as a paper in 1799, which reported on the skeletal remains of an extinct large sloth, which he named _[Megalonyx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalonyx "Megalonyx")_, unearthed by saltpeter workers from a cave in what is now Monroe County, West Virginia.[[426]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Babcock-2024-448)[[427]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-449) Jefferson is considered to be a pioneer of scientific paleontology research in North America.[[428]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-450)

Jefferson served as APS president for the next eighteen years, including through both terms of his presidency.[[424]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFAPS-446) He introduced [Meriwether Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriwether_Lewis "Meriwether Lewis") to the society, where various scientists tutored him in preparation for the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition "Lewis and Clark Expedition").[[424]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-TJFAPS-446)[[429]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Ambrose,_1996,_p._126-451) He resigned on January 20, 1815, but remained active through correspondence.[[430]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-452)

### Linguistics

Jefferson had a lifelong interest in [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics "Linguistics"), and could speak, read, and write in a number of languages, including French, Greek, Italian, and German. In his early years, he excelled in classical languages.[[431]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-ReferenceA-453)[[432]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-454) Jefferson later came to regard Greek as the "perfect language" as expressed in its laws and philosophy.[[433]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Boberchap1-455) While attending the College of William & Mary, he taught himself Italian.[[434]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Italy-456) Here Jefferson first became familiar with the [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon "Anglo-Saxon") language, studying it in a linguistic and philosophical capacity. He owned 17 volumes of Anglo-Saxon texts and grammar and later wrote an essay on the Anglo-Saxon language.[[431]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-ReferenceA-453) Jefferson claimed to have taught himself Spanish during his nineteen-day journey to France, using only a grammar guide and a copy of _[Don Quixote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don\_Quixote "Don Quixote")_.[[435]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-457)

Linguistics played a significant role in how Jefferson modeled and expressed political and philosophical ideas. He believed that the study of ancient languages was essential in understanding the roots of modern language.[[436]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-Hellen155-458) Jefferson criticized [language purists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_purism_in_English "Linguistic purism in English") and supported the introduction of neologisms to English, foreseeing the emergence of ["an American dialect"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English"). He described the [Académie Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_Fran%C3%A7aise "Académie Française"), a body designated to regulate the French language, as an "[endeavor to arrest the progress of their language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_prescription#Criticisms "Linguistic prescription")".[[437]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-neology-459)

He collected and understood a number of [Native American vocabularies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas "Indigenous languages of the Americas") and instructed Lewis and Clark to record and collect various indigenous languages during their Expedition.[[438]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-460) When Jefferson moved from Washington after his presidency, he took 50 Native American vocabulary lists back to Monticello along with the rest of his possessions. Somewhere along the journey, a thief stole the heavy chest, thinking it was full of valuables, but its contents were dumped into the James River when the thief discovered it was only filled with papers. Thirty years of collecting were lost, with only a few fragments rescued from the muddy banks of the river.[[439]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-461)

Jefferson was not an outstanding orator and preferred to communicate through writing or remain silent if possible. Instead of delivering his [State of the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Union "State of the Union") addresses himself, Jefferson wrote the annual messages and sent a representative to read them aloud in Congress, which started a tradition that continued until 1913, when President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson "Woodrow Wilson") chose to deliver his State of the Union address to Congress verbally and in person.[[440]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-462)

### Inventions

Jefferson invented many small practical devices and improved contemporary inventions, including a revolving book-stand and a "Great Clock" powered by the gravitational pull on cannonballs. He improved the [pedometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedometer "Pedometer"), the [polygraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygraph_(duplicating_device) "Polygraph (duplicating device)") (a device for duplicating writing),[[441]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-463) and the [moldboard plow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldboard_plow "Moldboard plow"), an idea he never patented and gave to posterity.[[442]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-464) Jefferson can also be credited as the creator of the [swivel chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swivel_chair#Origin "Swivel chair"), the first of which he created and used to write much of the Declaration of Independence.[[443]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-465) He first opposed patents but later supported them. From 1790 to 1793, as Secretary of State, he was the _ex officio_ head of the three-person patent review board. He drafted reforms of US patent law which led to him being relieved of this duty in 1793, and also drastically changed the patent system.[[444]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-466)

As Minister to France, Jefferson was impressed by the military standardization program known as the _[Système Gribeauval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gribeauval\_system "Gribeauval system")_, and initiated a program as president to develop [interchangeable parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchangeable_parts "Interchangeable parts") for firearms. For his inventiveness and ingenuity, Jefferson was awarded an honorary Doctor of Law degree from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1787.[[445]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-467)

## Legacy

### Historical reputation

Jefferson is seen as an icon of individual liberty, democracy, and [republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism "Republicanism"), hailed as the author of the Declaration of Independence, an architect of the American Revolution, and a [renaissance man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath#Renaissance_man "Polymath") who promoted science and scholarship.[[446]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-468) The participatory democracy and expanded suffrage he championed defined his era and became a standard for later generations.[[447]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-469)[Jon Meacham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Meacham "Jon Meacham") opined that Jefferson was the most influential figure of the democratic republic in its first half-century, succeeded by presidential adherents [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison "James Madison"), [James Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Monroe "James Monroe"), [Andrew Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson "Andrew Jackson"), and [Martin Van Buren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren "Martin Van Buren").[[448]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-470) A [Siena Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena_Research_Institute "Siena Research Institute") poll of presidential scholars, which began in 1982, has consistently ranked Jefferson as one of the five best U.S. presidents,[[449]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-471) and a 2015 [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution "Brookings Institution") poll of [American Political Science Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Political_Science_Association "American Political Science Association") members ranked him as the fifth-greatest president.[[450]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-472)

### Memorials and honors

Jefferson has been memorialized with buildings, sculptures, [postage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._presidents_on_U.S._postage_stamps "U.S. presidents on U.S. postage stamps"), and [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar"). In the 1920s, Jefferson, together with [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington"), [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt"), and [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln "Abraham Lincoln"), was chosen by sculptor [Gutzon Borglum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutzon_Borglum "Gutzon Borglum") and approved by President [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge "Calvin Coolidge") to be depicted in a stone national memorial at [Mount Rushmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rushmore "Mount Rushmore") in the [Black Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hills "Black Hills") in [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota "South Dakota").[[451]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-rushmore-473)

The [Jefferson Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Memorial "Jefferson Memorial") was dedicated in Washington, D.C., in 1943, on the 200th anniversary of Jefferson's birth. The interior of the memorial includes a 19-foot (6 m) statue of Jefferson by [Rudulph Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudulph_Evans "Rudulph Evans") and engravings of passages from Jefferson's writings. Most prominent among these passages are the words inscribed around the Jefferson Memorial: "I have sworn upon the altar of God eternal hostility against every form of tyranny over the mind of man", a quote from Jefferson's September 23, 1800, letter to [Benjamin Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Rush "Benjamin Rush").[[452]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-474)

In October 2021, in response to lobbying, the New York City Public Design Commission voted unanimously to remove the plaster model of [the statue of Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Thomas_Jefferson_(David_d%27Angers) "Statue of Thomas Jefferson (David d'Angers)") that currently stands in the [United States Capitol rotunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Capitol_rotunda "United States Capitol rotunda") from the chamber of the [New York City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council "New York City Council"), where it had been for more than a century, due to him fathering children with people he enslaved.[[461]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-483) The statue was taken down the next month.[[462]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson#cite_note-484)
